## What the paper can say
- With antipodal (V = 2) and orthogonal (V = 16) value codes the store is level with the least-squares associative memory
  over R1 on three of the four LLM families (Qwen3-8B, Llama, Mistral; intervals include 0) and behind it on Granite
  (-0.74) and on all three sentence encoders (-1.7 to -2.9).
- At V = 16 the store beats LAM on all four LLM families (+2.3 to +4.7 points); at V = 2 LAM beats the store on six of
  seven encoders (3.5-4.2 points on the LLMs), so the V = 2 deficit is not only the value codes (the referee's account
  explains about half of it: the codes recover +3.1 to +5.1 points of B49's 6.6-9.3 point V = 2 gap to LAM on the LLMs, leaving 3.5-4.2).
- Against the best of all ten dev-chosen families the store still loses over R1 (0 of 7 pass; 6 losses, Llama a tie
  within 1 point). Without LAM it leads on Qwen3-8B (+2.37) and Llama (+4.20) by the +-2 rule, and is within 2 points
  ahead on Granite and Mistral.
- Tuning budgets: the store searches 72 configurations per cell, LAM 48, BEST picks among 690 (+16 learned maps).
- B49's VSA numbers at V in {2, 16} are superseded by B53's (B49's random-code store is reported as such).

## What the paper must not say
- That the store beats, or is ahead of, the least-squares memory over R1 on any encoder (VSA ahead on 0 of 7).
- That LLM codes as a class favour the store over LAM (Granite is behind; the "level" encoders are ties, not wins).
- That the value codes explain the whole V = 2 loss to LAM, or that the fix makes the store competitive with BEST.
- Any useful-accuracy regime for the store (none within 64 bits per fact; R1 accuracies at V = 16 on the LLMs are 26-31% against an
  exact index at 76-82%).
