## Reading (written by hand after the generated part; reading rules in the prereg)

Setting. Every stored bit of every memory is a cell (a differential pair of PCM devices read as a sign) with i.i.d.
sign errors at the device model's measured 30-day rate x k (B34 showed the model's errors are i.i.d.; a re-read of 16
cells with the device model itself agrees, below), or with localized damage at a matched overall error rate. Each
memory is read at the smallest budget, in cells per stored pair with every stored bit counted (directory, parity,
codebook where stored), at which it reads >= 99.5% of the 4,776 probed RULER keys exactly; designs were tuned on
synthetic dev stores and the condition's error rate is known to every designer. The VSA reads regenerate their
codebook digitally from the value strings (0 cells; the price is N_values x d code bits and dot products per query).

1. H1, primary (the referee's question): TABLE WIN at every noise level. Error-corrected tables need 2-4x fewer cells
   per fact than the best VSA read: 19 vs 76 cells with no cell errors (k1), 27 vs 76 at k4 (2.1% cell errors), 54 vs
   128 at k8 (12.3%), 215 vs 431 at k16 (27.6%), 724 vs 1722 at k32 (37.4%) (Delta -8, -8, -6, -5, -4, -5 grid steps;
   store bootstrap k8 [-5, -4], k16 [-4, -3]). The winning codes: a BCH table (tuned (n, t), repetition only where
   needed) at low error rates, an LDPC table with the copies' votes combined into soft inputs at high ones (R = 1/3 at
   k8 and k16, 1/4 at k32). The best VSA read is V-last (each key's last value bound with a LAST code), which needs no
   threshold; the published read (V-pub, threshold 0.5) needs 108-861 cells up to k8 and never reaches 99.5% at k16 or
   k32; V-Snt (B34's argmax read) stalls at 98.8% from k16 on (multi-valued keys of the small stores).
2. The referee's capacity point holds. With no cell errors the VSA store needs 76 cells per fact (about 108 pairs per
   8,192-cell vector; 0.17 value bits per cell) where a table needs 19 (0.66) and the directory-free capacity bound is
   13.5. The RULER stores at 16 pairs per vector (512 cells per fact) were over-provisioned 6.7x; B34's exactness at
   low precision was a property of that over-provisioning. The VSA's distance from the capacity bound (5.6x at k1, 5.8x
   at k32) is about twice the best table's (1.4x at k1, 2.5x at k32) at every noise level.
3. Against repetition alone the VSA store does need fewer cells at high noise (T-rep 256 / 1024 / 3444 vs 128 / 431 /
   1722 at k8 / k16 / k32): B34's comparison was against a repetition-coded table, and its VSA advantage (V-Snt +7 to
   +61 points at 512 cells) is an advantage over repetition, not over coding. A hashed CAM in BCH (E-CAM) pays for its
   26-bit key hashes (45-3444 cells).
4. H2: a table whose directory and values are random high-dimensional codes decoded by similarity, without
   superposition (T-vhdd), needs fewer cells than the VSA store at every level (32 / 38 / 91 / 304 / 1024 vs 76 / 76 /
   128 / 431 / 1722; Delta -2 to -5). Superposition costs cells; it buys none.
5. H1cb, item memory counted: a VSA whose value codebook (N_values x d) is stored on the same cells needs 431 cells at
   k1 to 1448 at k8 and never reaches 99.5% at k16 or k32 (load 1-2 pairs per vector chosen, i.e. hardly a VSA); 11-19
   grid steps (7-27x) above the best table. The VSA's competitiveness above depends on regenerating its codebook.
6. H3 (localized damage with an interleaver, unknown locations): TABLE WIN in all 12 conditions (Delta -2 to -6).
   With a random interleaver dead lines, stuck clusters and even a contiguous lost band look like i.i.d. errors to
   every memory: V-last, T-rep, T-vhdd and E-CAM stay within one grid step of their i.i.d. requirement in 12 of 12
   conditions, E-BCH and E-LDPC in 10 of 12 (E-BCH 91 vs 38 at lines/e05: the small stores' tables are single
   codewords, and localized damage on a tiny array varies a lot; hand-read below). H5 (locations known, erasures):
   TABLE WIN in all 4.
7. H4 (no interleaver, natural layout): VSA WIN in the 8 lines/cluster conditions. The VSA store keeps its i.i.d.
   requirement within 0-3 grid steps (V-last 76 / 91 / 152 / 512 for dead lines at f = 4 / 10 / 25 / 55% and 76 / 91 /
   152 / 724 for clusters) while every table needs at least 4 grid steps more than in its own i.i.d. condition (best
   non-HD table 181 to > 4096 cells; T-vhdd 91 to 2896). The mechanism is superposition: each fact is spread over the d = 8,192 cells of its shard vector, so a dead
   word line (256 cells) or a 16 x 16 cluster removes a small share of every fact in that vector, whereas a table
   keeps each fact (and, with copies adjacent, all copies of a bit) in a few contiguous cells, and a coded table
   concentrates the damage in some codewords (hand-read: 93-167 errors in codewords designed for 59). This is the
   one place where the VSA store needs fewer cells than every table, and it is specific to superposition: T-vhdd,
   with the same random codes but no superposition, fails like the other tables. But an interleaver gives the tables
   the same property at no cost in cells (point 6). Contiguous loss of a fraction f in the natural layout defeats every
   memory (whole vectors or codewords lost; no arm reaches 99.5% at any f up to 4,096 cells); at 512 cells the VSA
   reads 1 - f of its keys or slightly more (96.0 / 90.1 / 76.8 / 48.2%), 5-31 points more than the coded tables
   (T-rep, E-BCH, E-LDPC, T-vhdd), whose contiguous codewords each lose several facts at once.
   Exploratory (not registered): the ldpc package's belief propagation, the run's LDPC decoder, decodes far fewer words
   than our own sum-product decoder when stuck cells make the channel inputs confidently wrong (lines-nat-k8 at 512

   two agree on 602 of 604 words and decode the same ones. With our decoder, LDPC tables reach 99.5% at about 128

   lines at f = 55% (VSA 512) (octave grid; results/b38_explore_ldpc_own.txt). The VSA WIN in H4 stands, with smaller
   margins at f = 4% (about 3 grid steps instead of > 17).
8. H6 (graceful degradation at equal cells, designs fixed for k8 at 128 cells per fact): the coded table degrades
   later but abruptly - E-LDPC reads 100 / 99.4 / 98.4% at eps = 0.16 / 0.20 / 0.24 against V-last 98.7 / 94.8 / 84.0,

   the VSA (70.4% at 0.276): the gradual failure belongs to similarity decoding of per-fact random codes, not to
   superposition. Without an interleaver (lines, natural layout) the VSA is ahead at every eps (+13 to +43 points).
9. H7 (under-provisioned memories, i.i.d.): below the best table's N* the VSA reads more keys at 37 of 49 budgets,

   these are inexact memories (well below 99.5%), and T-vhdd reads as many or more (70.4% at k16/128).

natural layout the failing keys' shards had more damaged cells (0.13-0.23 vs 0.125 overall). E-BCH failures are keys
with one codeword beyond 2t; E-LDPC below N* fails almost every codeword at once (99 of 101 not converged); T-vhdd and
T-rep failures are keys whose value code or majority falls.

Failed predictions: P7 (V-last at 512 cells under contiguous loss read 48.2% at f = 55%, 3.4 points above 1 - f
instead of within 3; its other parts held), P9 (E-LDPC was ahead of the VSA by 4.6 points at eps = 0.20, not >= 5; the

model 0 flags). 8 of 10 mechanical predictions held, P11 counted separately.

Deviations and disclosures. (a) Gap fill: the registered extension rule (b38_design.py) extended windows whose top
read below 99.5% or whose N* sat at the bottom; it missed N* values sitting above unevaluated budgets (e.g. E-BCH at
lines-il-e05 reported 512 with 91-431 unevaluated). After the first test pass I added a gap-filling step (fill every
budget between the highest failing budget below N* and N*), in the spirit of the registered "until bracketed", and
ran it once (113 evaluations); the tables above include it, and it changed 8 N* values, all downward and all tables
in damage conditions (E-BCH lines-il-e05 512 -> 91, lines-nat-k4 512 -> 181, lines-nat-e05 512 -> 431, clust-nat-k4
512 -> 215; E-CAM lines-nat-k4 512 -> 304; T-vhdd clust-nat-k4 512 -> 91, clust-nat-e05 512 -> 128, clust-nat-k8
512 -> 215); no bar label and no prediction outcome changed. (b) The exploratory own-decoder LDPC runs (point 7) are not registered.
(c) The first full test pass checkpointed its results every 25 jobs, which slowed its tail; I/O only.

Limits. One published PCM model, hard (sign) reads, single-level cells, 30-day errors; localized damage as stuck
random signs with three geometries; stored cells only - decoder logic, the VSA's N_values x d similarity computation
per query, interleaver wiring and read energy are not counted (an interleaved table touches cells all over the array
for one fact; the VSA reads one contiguous vector). Every design knows the overall cell-error rate. The LDPC codes are
our own IRA constructions at <= 1,024 information bits (high-rate codes of this length have error floors) and the
run's decoder is weaker than ours under mismatched inputs, so the LDPC requirements are upper bounds; better codes
would widen the table's lead. V-am's c (0.9) and V-cb's load (1-2) sit at the edge of their dev grids.

What the paper can say (numbers from this file; shares of the 4,776 probed RULER keys; 30-day device statistics):
- "Read at the smallest budget at which each memory is still exact (>= 99.5% of keys), with every stored bit on the
  same simulated phase-change cells, tables protected by BCH or LDPC codes need 2-4x fewer cells per fact than the VSA
  store at every noise level we tested: 19 vs 76 cells with no cell errors, 54 vs 128 at a 12% cell-error rate, 724 vs
  1,722 at 37%. The VSA store's exactness at 16 pairs per 8,192-dimensional vector reflected 6.7-fold
  over-provisioning."
- "A table of random high-dimensional codes decoded by similarity, without superposition, also needs fewer cells than
  the VSA store (1.4-2.4x); superposition costs storage. A VSA whose codebook is stored on the cells needs 7-27x more
  cells than the coded table."
- "Superposition does buy one property: because each fact is spread over a whole vector, the VSA store stored without an
  interleaver tolerates dead word lines and stuck-cell clusters at the same cell count it needs for random errors,
  while tables, whose codewords are local, need several times more cells or fail. An interleaver gives tables the same
  property at no cost in cells."
- "When a memory is under-provisioned or the error rate exceeds its design point, the VSA store loses facts gradually
  (e.g. 66% of keys at 27.6% cell errors with 128 cells per fact, where an LDPC table designed for 12% reads 13%), a
  property shared by tables of random codes decoded by similarity."
- Method: an equal-cell comparison with BCH and LDPC baselines, "we found no precedent" (results/b38_literature.txt).

What the paper must not say:
- That the VSA store is more storage-efficient or more robust to device noise than an error-corrected table, at any
  noise level (H1 TABLE WIN at every k); B34's "VSA beats every non-HD table at x16 / x32" holds against repetition only.
- That VSA robustness to localized damage is an advantage over ECC in general: it holds only against tables stored
  without an interleaver (H3 TABLE WIN with one; H5 TABLE WIN when damage locations are known), and contiguous loss
  defeats the VSA store as much as any table.
- That graceful degradation is specific to superposition (T-vhdd degrades as gracefully), or that it helps at the
  99.5% exactness bar (it is a property of inexact memories).
- That the comparison covers decoder logic, read energy or area (stored cells only), or multi-level cells / analog reads.
- "First" (no precedent found).
