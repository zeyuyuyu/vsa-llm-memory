   or dropped articles, full or partial names), as in B60's material; the plain-loop scorer checks the counts.
2. The lowest cells (Qwen3-8B M = 256 V = 16 8 bits per fact, -5.71; e5 M = 1,024 V = 16 16 bits per fact, -7.83) are
   sixteen-value cells, as in B60. The queries only NLMSc reads are mostly near-copies of the label ('Runner' / 'The
   Runner', 'Norwegen' / 'Norway', 'York City' / 'York City F.C.', 'Prophet' / 'A Prophet'), which the store misreads
   through cross-talk; LAMc gives the same answer as NLMSc in 5 of 10 (Qwen3-8B) and 7 of 10 (e5). Among the queries
   only the store reads, LAMc reads 6 of 10 correctly on Qwen3-8B (losses of the online write itself: the batch
   solve would have read them) and 1 of 10 on e5, where NLMSc and LAMc give the same wrong answer in 6 of 10 (e.g.
   'Seljuq Sultanate' / Sultanate of Rum, 'Jeanne III' / Jeanne d'Albret, 'Yaaba' / Grandmother: distant aliases,
   errors of the least-squares solution rather than of the online write).
3. The highest cells are where the store is ahead of NLMSc in one condition: Qwen3-8B M = 1,024 V = 16 4 bits per fact
   (+2.23; NLMSc there wrote only p = 224 features for 1,024 facts and chose the Polyak average) and e5 M = 1,024 V = 2
   16 bits per fact (+1.75; NLMSc on 16,320 random features). In both, the two sets of single-arm successes are of the
   same kind (near-copies and abbreviations on both sides), consistent with noise between two memories of similar
   accuracy in that cell rather than a class of query that one write handles and the other does not.
