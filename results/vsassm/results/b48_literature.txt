B48 literature check (prereg/B48_one_form_and_alias_naming_prereg.md), written after the B48 results. Each entry:
the B48 finding it bears on, and whether the source supports it, conflicts with it, or has no direct precedent.
Sources were looked up on 2026-11-19 (abstracts / proceedings pages; full texts not re-read for this note).

PART B (naming where identity is not in the surface string)

1. Wu, Petroni, Josifoski, Riedel, Zettlemoyer (2020), "Scalable Zero-shot Entity Linking with Dense Entity Retrieval"
   (BLINK), EMNLP 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03814 . A bi-encoder trained on some entities links mentions to
   entities never seen in training. B48: a bilinear pointer trained on training entities names unseen entities' aliases
   from frozen Qwen3-8B states at 0.661 (W) and pronouns at 0.827 (P). SUPPORTS that a trained mention-to-entity matcher
   can generalise to unseen entities; B48's head is far weaker than BLINK (no entity descriptions, frozen states, 800
   training stories), so no number is comparable.

2. Mallen et al. (2023), "When Not to Trust Language Models: Investigating Effectiveness of Parametric and
   Non-Parametric Memories" (PopQA), ACL 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10511 . LMs know facts about popular
   entities and not about the long tail; scaling does little for the tail. B48's W entities and aliases are PopQA
   subjects; the head names well-known aliases (AK -> Alaska, Un prophete -> A Prophet, The Addicks -> Charlton
   Athletic) and fails on obscure ones (foreign release titles, ICAO codes such as FYRU). SUPPORTS (identity from world
   knowledge is available only where the LM knows the alias).

3. De Cao, Izacard, Riedel, Petroni (2021), "Autoregressive Entity Retrieval" (GENRE), ICLR 2021,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904 . A seq2seq model fine-tuned to generate an entity's unique name links mentions
   well. B48: prompted zero-shot, Qwen3-8B mostly copies a W alias as written (76% of alias mentions) instead of writing
   the listed name (17%). NO DIRECT PRECEDENT for the zero-shot setting (GENRE is fine-tuned with constrained decoding);
   not a conflict.

4. Ding, Zeng, Weninger (2024), "ChatEL: Entity Linking with Chatbots", https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14858 ; Liu et al.
   (2024), "OneNet: A Fine-Tuning Free Framework for Few-Shot Entity Linking via Large Language Model Prompting", EMNLP
   2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07549 . Prompted LLMs link entities when the task is posed with candidates and
   multi-step prompting (candidate generation, disambiguation as a choice). B48's single free-form prompt ("write the
   listed name it refers to") elicited copying. SUPPORTS the reading that the LM arm's W result is a property of this
   prompt, not a ceiling of prompted LMs; a candidate-choice prompt was not run in B48.

5. Feng & Steinhardt (2024), "How do Language Models Bind Entities in Context?", ICLR 2024,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191 ; Kim & Schuster (2023), "Entity Tracking in Language Models", ACL 2023,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02363 . In-context binding is carried in the activations (binding-ID vectors); entity
   tracking surfaces unevenly across models. B48: the same LM resolves P pronouns when it writes text (0.998) but a
   bilinear read-out of layer-18 span means reaches 0.827, and its errors pick a same-gender listed name. SUPPORTS that
   the identity is present in the LM's computation; NO DIRECT PRECEDENT for how much of it a low-rank pointer on
   span-mean states recovers.

6. Tenney et al. (2019), "BERT Rediscovers the Classical NLP Pipeline", ACL 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05950 .
   Edge probes recover coreference from frozen contextual states, less well than syntactic labels. SUPPORTS the partial
   (0.83) pronoun naming from frozen states.

PART A (one surface form per symbol)

7. The B42 literature (results/b42_literature.txt: VQ-VAE / EMA codebooks, Gumbel-softmax, straight-through estimators,
   the discrete key-value bottleneck, codebook collapse, anisotropy and centring / whitening of LM states) applies
   unchanged: B48 reruns B42's seven interfaces with B42's grids on arena C1. B48 adds that removing the second written
   form does not change the outcome (all seven FAIL on both encoders, best recovery 0.053), and that the codes follow
   the mention's position in the input (the query mention gets one key and the candidate another, whatever the symbol)
   rather than its written symbol. The anisotropy literature (e.g. Ethayarajh 2019, "How Contextual are Contextualized
   Word Representations?", EMNLP 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00512 : contextual states of the same word in
   different contexts are far apart in upper layers) SUPPORTS that identical written forms do not give matching
   final-layer states, which a label-free clusterer would need. NO PRECEDENT found for the specific test (label-free
   discrete codes from frozen LM states feeding a two-hop VSA chain with one written form per symbol).

8. Exact string hashing over hand-written spans reaching the exact-symbol ceiling (C1 1.000, C 0.526) is a property of
   the task construction (identity in the written form), not a literature claim; reported as a check.
