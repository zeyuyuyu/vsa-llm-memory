## Notes (by me, after reading the 40 queries above)
- In both encoders the queries only LAM answers right are mostly ones the exact index also gets right (qwen8 546 of
  720, 76%; e5 613 of 837, 73%), and the sampled ones are plain aliases with a clearly matching label ("S. Korea" ->
  "South Korea", "N.S.W." -> "New South Wales", "City of Angels" -> "Los Angeles", "West Visayas" -> "Western Visayas").
  The similarity is there; the superposed read loses it in cross-talk from the other stored keys, which the
  least-squares memory removes by inverting the stored keys' Gram matrix (its read is decorrelated, the store's is
  Hebbian). This is the expected difference between a pseudo-inverse and an outer-product memory, not a code effect.
- The queries only the VSA answers right have a lower exact-index rate (qwen8 276 of 449, 61%; e5 291 of 490, 59%):
  at V = 2 a read that misses the entity still lands on the right value half of the time, so part of these wins are
  chance ("Verse" -> "Benjamin Mount", "The Radical" -> "Katherine", "Son Kain" -> "Gain"); the rest are good aliases
  where LAM's quantized ridge read failed ("Republic of Ghana" -> "Ghana", "netminder" -> "goalkeeper").
- No sign of a scoring or alignment fault: aliases, labels and gold values line up with the stores.
