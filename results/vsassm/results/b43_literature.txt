1. Kim & Schuster 2023, boxes task
Citation: Najoung Kim, Sebastian Schuster. "Entity Tracking in Language Models." ACL 2023. arXiv 2305.02363 (v2, 8 Sep 2023,
  arXiv comment "ACL 2023 Camera-ready").
Claims:
 - VERIFIED design: world W=(O,n,m,e) with n=7 boxes, m=3 max objects per box, e=2 expected objects per box at the start,
   |O|=100 nouns (BNC frequency >27). Each scenario = initial state + 12 operations (NumOps fixed at 12); 2,200 scenarios
   sampled, only valid operations. Operations: put in, take out, move between boxes; deliberately no "empty the box" op.
 - VERIFIED templated: "The initial state and the operations were converted into naturalistic descriptions using
   predefined templates." Task: after 0-12 operations, cloze "Box N contains __"; one test item per box after each
   operation = n x (NumOps+1) = 91 items per scenario. Initial-state "signatures" are kept disjoint between train and eval.
 - VERIFIED variants: AltForms (disjoint phrasings, Table 2), AmbiRef (adjective-modified objects resolved only by the
   current state), MoveContents (move a whole box's contents). Fine-tuning splits: Base, NumOps (train <=2 ops, test up
   to 12), Vocab (disjoint object names), AltForms, AltForms+NumOps, AmbiRef, MoveContents.
 - VERIFIED in-context (2 demos; davinci, text-davinci-003, Flan-T5; strong random baseline of 0-3 co-mentioned
   objects): only text-davinci-003 consistently beat the baseline ("all contents of a box after 7 operations in more
   than 25% of the cases"); Flan-T5 mostly copied the initial state; code-trained GPT-3.5 models (incl.
   code-davinci-002, no RLHF) beat GPT-3 -> the code-pretraining hypothesis. Fine-tuned T5-base is near-perfect on Base,
   degrades on NumOps/AltForms/AmbiRef/MoveContents; random-init T5 fails.
 - VERIFIED reanalysis of Li et al. 2021 (Alchemy): 87.6% of probe items are trivial (unchanged or emptied beaker); only
   12.4% test real state change; always-predict-initial-state baseline = 62.7%.
 - VERIFIED dataset access (README of github.com/sebschu/entity-tracking-lms): data/boxes-dataset-v1.zip is
   password-protected "to prevent leakage of the dataset into the training data of future language models"; the password
   (iamnotaLM) is printed in the README; model-outputs/model-outputs.zip uses the same one; the README asks users not to put
   the uncompressed files in any repository. The README does not describe the variants/splits.
 - VERIFIED (abstract only) follow-up: Tang, Zhao, Franco, Wijaya, Mueller, Schuster, Kim, "Do Language Models Track
   Entities Across State Changes?", arXiv 2605.30233, ICML 2026: LMs "do not incrementally track world states across
   tokens ... but simply aggregate relevant information in parallel at the last token"; REMOVE is implemented by a
   "fragile global suppression tag" that predicts failure modes.
Bearing on B43: boxes is templated synthetic text, so it is a clean contamination-guarded probe but not "natural text";
  ProPara has to carry the natural-text claim. Keep the unzipped data out of any repo or released deposit.

2. Kim, Schuster & Toshniwal 2024
Citation: Najoung Kim, Sebastian Schuster, Shubham Toshniwal. "Code Pretraining Improves Entity Tracking Abilities of
  Language Models." arXiv 2405.21068 (v1 only, 31 May 2024). Venue: none on arXiv (no comment or journal-ref); Semantic
  Scholar lists arXiv only. Treat as a preprint (any peer-reviewed venue UNVERIFIED).
Claims (VERIFIED):
 - Base vs +code pairs (Llama 2/Code Llama 7-70B, DeepSeek/-Coder 7B, Gemma/CodeGemma 8B), +math pairs (Llemma,
   DeepSeek-Math, FLoat, OpenMathMistral) and alignment pairs (Chat/Instruct). Task: boxes "base" version, "seven boxes
   followed by 1-12 descriptions of operations"; constrained decoding (outlines) because small models broke the format.
 - Findings: "clear evidence that models additionally trained on large amounts of code outperform the base models"; "no
   consistent benefit of additional math training or alignment tuning"; base models gain more from alignment tuning than
   code models; best model CodeLlama 70B-instruct, 64.9 accuracy on items with 1+ operations. Gains for Gemma and the
   DeepSeek pair are smaller, in line with scale / amount of code.
Bearing on B43: Qwen3-8B is heavily code-trained, so the direct-LM arm on boxes is not a straw man. For scale, the best
  open model in 2024 reached 64.9% on 1+-operation items.

3. ProPara, the document-level metric, and reported numbers
Citations:
 a) Bhavana Dalvi Mishra, Lifu Huang, Niket Tandon, Wen-tau Yih, Peter Clark. "Tracking State Changes in Procedural Text: a
    Challenge Dataset and Models for Process Paragraph Comprehension." NAACL 2018. arXiv 1805.06975. (Usually cited as
    Dalvi et al. 2018; "Mishra et al." is the same paper.)
 b) Niket Tandon, Bhavana Dalvi Mishra, Joel Grus, Wen-tau Yih, Antoine Bosselut, Peter Clark. "Reasoning about Actions and
    State Changes by Injecting Commonsense Knowledge" (ProStruct). EMNLP 2018. arXiv 1808.10012.
Claims:
 - VERIFIED (a): 488 crowd-written paragraphs about 183 processes (paragraphs of <=4 sentences dropped); 81,345
   annotations; experts set participants and create/destroy steps, Turkers filled locations (span or "unknown"), 5 grids
   per paragraph, average pairwise agreement 0.67; 80/10/10 split by process prompt (test processes unseen).
 - VERIFIED split (downloaded allenai/propara data/emnlp18/grids.v1.{train,dev,test}.json): 391/43/54 paragraphs;
   1,504/175/236 participants; 2,639/290/373 sentences; naacl18.partition.paraid.tsv agrees, as do KG-MRC Table 1, CGLI
   Table 2 and the leaderboard README ("Evaluated 54 predictions against 54 answers"). Inconsistent: CoCoGen Table 12
   (387/43/54) and IWCS 2025 ("42 paragraphs out of 52 of the original ProPara test set").
 - VERIFIED metric (b, Sec 6.1): four templated questions per process: Inputs (exist at the start but not at the end),
   Outputs (not at the start, exist at the end), Conversions (4-tuple convert-from, convert-to, location, sentence-id) and
   Moves (4-tuple entity, from-location, to-location, sentence-id). Each question gets its own F1; tuples are paired by
   sentence-id and scored by Hamming distance; the four F1s are macro-averaged. Items to predict, train/dev/test:
   7043/913/1095.
 - VERIFIED evaluator README (aristo-leaderboard/propara/evaluator): overall P = (avg IP + avg OP + avg CP + avg MP)/4,
   same for R, overall F1 = harmonic mean of P and R. Locations are lower-cased, a leading article is stripped, then Porter
   stemmed; participants are not normalized. Action column is NONE/CREATE/MOVE/DESTROY; "?" = unknown, "-" = does not
   exist. No version number is given.
 - VERIFIED (b) Table 1, test P/R/F1: ProLocal 77.4/22.9/35.3, ProGlobal 46.7/52.4/49.4, ProStruct 74.2/42.1/53.7.
 - VERIFIED evaluator drift: leaderboard-sourced tables (KOALA Table 2 says so; CGLI copies it) give ProLocal 50.7,
   ProGlobal 51.9, ProStruct 74.3/43.0/54.5, KG-MRC 57.6 (KOALA) vs 56.8 in the KG-MRC paper.
 - VERIFIED fine-tuned document-level test P/R/F1, each read in its own paper:
     KG-MRC (Das et al., ICLR 2019, arXiv 1810.05682)      64.52/50.68/56.77
     NCET (Gupta & Durrett 2019, arXiv 1904.03518)         64.2/53.9/58.6; +ELMo 67.1/58.5/62.5
       (venue: NAACL 2019 SPNLP workshop per Semantic Scholar, UNVERIFIED)
     DynaPro (Amini et al., AKBC 2020, arXiv 2003.13878)   75.2/58.0/65.5
     TSLM (Rajaby Faghihi & Kordjamshidi, NAACL 2021, arXiv 2104.07635)   68.4/68.9/68.6
     KOALA (Zhang, Geng, Qin, Wu, Jiang; WWW 2021, not AAAI; arXiv 2009.13199)   77.7/64.4/70.4
     CGLI (Ma, Ilievski, Francis, Nyberg, Oltramari; COLING 2022, arXiv 2208.12848)   74.9/70.0/72.4; +data aug. 75.7/70.0/72.7
     MeeT (Singh, Bai, Wang; EACL 2023, arXiv 2210.06444; fine-tuned T5)   80.3/67.1/73.1  <- best fine-tuned number I verified
   UNVERIFIED: LEMON (Shi et al. 2022) 74.8/69.8/72.2, quoted only in MeeT's Table 1.
 - VERIFIED LLM-prompted numbers:
   * CoCoGen: Aman Madaan, Shuyan Zhou, Uri Alon, Yiming Yang, Graham Neubig. "Language Models of Code are Few-Shot
     Commonsense Learners." EMNLP 2022. arXiv 2210.07128. ProPara is written as Python: one state_k variable per
     participant, one function per sentence that reassigns every state ("UNK" = unknown, None = does not exist). 3-shot,
     mean of 5 random prompts (Table 3): curie 95.1/22.3/36.1, davinci 75.5/47.1/58.0, CoCoGen (code-davinci-002)
     80.0/53.6/63.0; "would be ranked 6th" on the leaderboard as of 10/11/2022. Caution: the metric is described only as
     "follow Dalvi et al. (2018) and measure precision, recall and F1 score of the predicted entity states", so it is not
     clearly the aristo document-level evaluator.
   * Ye, Obiso, Tu, Pustejovsky. "ProPara-CRTS: Canonical Referent Tracking for Reliable Evaluation of Entity State
     Tracking in Process Narratives." IWCS 2025, pp. 254-268 (ACL Anthology 2025.iwcs-main.22). GPT-4o, ProPara test,
     sentence/document F1 (Table 2): Direct 37.1/59.6; CoT 38.4/60.8; Self-consistency (8 samples) 40.2/62.1; Few-shot
     39.0/61.3. Table 3: GPT-4o-mini 24.8/55.0; Llama-3.1-8B LoRA 59.5/64.9; their re-runs of MeeT 54.9/69.4 and CGLI
     65.4/72.4. Prompts are per participant per step (6-label state + location extraction), paragraph cut at step t, so
     not a full-grid prompt. The re-annotation fixed 789 errors (Table 1), "approximately 11%" of the annotations.
Bearing on B43: reference points on the document-level metric are GPT-4o prompted 59.6-62.1, code-davinci-002 3-shot
  63.0, fine-tuned SOTA 72-73. Report the evaluator version; label noise means no arm should be expected near 100.

4. OpenPI and OpenPI 2.0
Citations: Niket Tandon, Keisuke Sakaguchi, Bhavana Dalvi Mishra, Dheeraj Rajagopal, Peter Clark, Michal Guerquin, Kyle
  Richardson, Eduard Hovy. "A Dataset for Tracking Entities in Open Domain Procedural Text." EMNLP 2020. arXiv 2011.08092
  (NB: arXiv 2010.02311 is a different paper). Li Zhang, Hainiu Xu, Abhinav Kommula, Chris Callison-Burch, Niket Tandon.
  "OpenPI2.0: An Improved Dataset for Entity Tracking in Texts." EACL 2024. arXiv 2305.14603.
Claims (VERIFIED):
 - OpenPI: input is only the procedural text; output per step is a set of (entity, attribute, before-state, after-state)
   tuples, "where the entity, attribute, and state values must be predicted from an open vocabulary". 29,928 state
   changes over 4,050 sentences from 810 WikiHow paragraphs; 91.5% coverage judged by humans; the best generation model
   reached 16.1% F1 under a BLEU-based metric.
 - OpenPI2.0: canonicalizes (clusters) entities and attributes and adds salience; exact-match F1 / BERTScore. Schemata
   (global) exact F1: gpt-3.5-turbo .151, text-davinci-003 .362, LLaMA 65B .129; state accuracy .074/.225/.102.
Bearing on B43: the attributes and values are free-form strings, so there is no closed state space and no exact-match
  read rule; a key-value store comparison would need canonicalization first. This is the reason to use ProPara, where
  the state space is existence plus a location span.

5. Chain-of-Agents
Citation: Yusen Zhang, Ruoxi Sun, Yanfei Chen, Tomas Pfister, Rui Zhang, Sercan O. Arik. "Chain of Agents: Large Language
  Models Collaborating on Long-Context Tasks." NeurIPS 2024 (main track). arXiv 2406.02818. arXiv has only v1, which lacks
  the camera-ready appendices. There is no "Goldberg" among the authors.
Claims (VERIFIED in arXiv v1 Table 9 and the NeurIPS camera-ready Table 11; the wording is identical):
 - Worker W_i prompt: "{Input Chunk c_i} / Here is the summary of the previous source text: {Previous Communication Unit
   (CU_i-1)} / Question: {Query q} / You need to read current source text and summary of previous source text (if any)
   and generate a summary to include them both. Later, this summary will be used for other agents to answer the Query,
   if any. So please write the summary that can include the evidence for answering the Query:"
 - Manager prompt: "{Task specific requirement} / The following are given passages. However, the source text is too long
   and has been summarized. You need to answer based on the summary: / {Previous Communication Unit CU_l} / Question:
   {question} / Answer:". Algorithm 1: chunks shorter than window k, CU_0 = empty string, the same LLM serves as workers
   and manager unless stated otherwise.
 - Settings: default window 8k ("due to the limitation of text-bison and unicorn"); temperature 0 except the
   self-consistency runs; max generation 1024 tokens (2048 for gemini-ultra). Backbones: text-bison, text-unicorn
   (PaLM 2), gemini-ultra, claude-3-haiku/sonnet/opus (llama3-8b only for timing, Sec 5.7). Datasets: HotpotQA, MuSiQue,
   NarrativeQA, Qasper, QuALITY, QMSum, GovReport, BookSum, RepoBench-P. Baselines: Vanilla, RAG (300-word chunks),
   Merge, Hierarchical.
 - Camera-ready only: App. F.3 runs NIAH PLUS (following LongAgent); text-bison rises from 26.0% (Vanilla 8k) to
   97.8% (CoA 8k), with Figure 7 token limits of 1,000-128,000. App. D: Claude 3 Haiku, windows 4k-128k on NarrativeQA,
   rises up to 16k, then holds near 20. App. F.4 "catastrophic collapse": rejection cues in CU_i-1 ("I don't know the
   answer to the query", "not mentioned") are repeated by later workers, and the chain can fall below a single agent W_1.
 - Neither version evaluates RULER or BABILong (full-text search). The checklist says "We will provide open access to
   the data and code upon acceptance"; whether an official repo exists is UNVERIFIED.
Bearing on B43: run CoA with the Table 11 prompts verbatim, temperature 0, 8k chunks and one model for both roles.
  Report how often chains collapse (rejection phrases in CU). A low CoA score on RULER NIAH has to be squared with the
  authors' own 97.8% on NIAH PLUS.

6. LLM x MapReduce (and V2)
Citation: Zihan Zhou, Chong Li, Xinyi Chen, Shuo Wang, Yu Chao, Zhili Li, Haoyu Wang, Rongqiao An, Qi Shi, Zhixing Tan,
  Xu Han, Xiaodong Shi, Zhiyuan Liu, Maosong Sun. "LLMxMapReduce: Simplified Long-Sequence Processing using Large Language
  Models." ACL 2025 (Long Papers), pp. 27664-27678 (2025.acl-long.1341). arXiv 2410.09342 (arXiv comment: "Work in
  Progress"). I downloaded the ACL PDF; its Table 1 matches arXiv v1.
Claims (VERIFIED):
 - Method: chunk -> map (structured output with confidence) -> collapse -> reduce, with in-context confidence
   calibration for conflicts. Llama3-70B-Instruct (8K effective) and Qwen2-72B-Instruct, vLLM, temperature 0.7.
 - InfiniteBench (Code.Run and Math.Calc excluded), Table 1, Re.PassKey / Re.Number / Re.KV / Avg:
     GPT-4 100/100/89.00/57.34; Qwen2-72B-Instruct 100/100/29.00/54.74
     L3-70B-I + LongAgent 99.32/93.05/74.60/53.81
     L3-70B-I + CoA        9.32/15.59/ 1.80/15.97   <- third-party CoA re-run collapses on synthetic retrieval
     L3-70B-I x MR        100/99.79/98.89/68.66;  Q2-72B-I x MR 100/100/98.80/65.97
 - NIAH extended to 1280K tokens with Llama3-70B-Instruct (Figure 3; no table). No RULER or BABILong results.
 - V2 (Haoyu Wang, Yujia Fu, Zhu Zhang, ..., Maosong Sun; arXiv 2504.05732) targets long-form article generation from
   long sources (SurveyEval benchmark). It has no retrieval or state-tracking results and is not relevant to B43.
Bearing on B43: the only third-party CoA run on synthetic retrieval I found gives 1.8% on KV retrieval. A weak CoA arm in
  B43 would match this, but we must show it is not an implementation artifact (check for collapse) before using it as
  evidence.

8. Store / symbolic-state vs direct LM on state tracking
 a) IC-DST: Yushi Hu, Chia-Hsuan Lee, Tianbao Xie, Tao Yu, Noah A. Smith, Mari Ostendorf. "In-Context Learning for
    Few-Shot Dialogue State Tracking." Findings of EMNLP 2022. arXiv 2203.08568. VERIFIED: Codex writes each turn's state
    changes as SQL, accumulated into the state. Table 3 (MultiWOZ 2.4, 5% data, JGA): full context -> full state 45.0;
    prev. state + turn -> full state 47.9; full context -> changes 52.0; single turn -> changes 55.8; prev. state + turn ->
    changes 58.7. Fig. 5: full-state JGA falls in later turns, change JGA stays high.
    Bearing: the closest earlier evidence that "LM extracts updates + deterministic accumulation" beats "LM outputs the full
    state" (+10.8 JGA). B43's store arm has the same structure, so do not claim novelty for update extraction itself.
 b) PROPOLIS: Hossein Rajaby Faghihi, Parisa Kordjamshidi, Choh Man Teng, James Allen. "The Role of Semantic Parsing in
    Understanding Procedural Text." Findings of EACL 2023. arXiv 2302.06829. VERIFIED (Table 4, ProPara test): PROPOLIS, a
    symbolic system with no training (TRIPS deep semantic parse + rules), scores 70.9/50.0/58.7 at document level vs
    ProLocal 35.3, ProGlobal 49.4, KG-MRC 56.8. NCET+PROPOLIS 74.6/65.8/69.9; CGLI+TRIPS 71.4 (CGLI 72.4).
    Bearing: a symbolic state tracker has already been scored on ProPara with this metric. B43 differs in using an LLM as
    the extractor and a VSA/dict store with fixed read rules.
 c) CoCoGen (item 3) already writes ProPara states as code, but the LM writes the full state at every step; nothing is
    executed and there are no deltas.
 d) Ruoyao Wang, Graham Todd, Ziang Xiao, Xingdi Yuan, Marc-Alexandre Cote, Peter Clark, Peter Jansen. "Can Language
    Models Serve as Text-Based World Simulators?" ACL 2024. arXiv 2406.06485. VERIFIED: on ByteSized32-State-Prediction,
    GPT-4 "does not exceed 59.9%" on transitions with a non-trivial change. Table 2, full vs diff prediction, whole
    transition F: human rules dynamic 59.9 vs 51.6, static 63.5 vs 73.9; no rules dynamic 54.1 vs 52.2; LLM rules
    dynamic 59.0 vs 59.5. (Human rules F_act 77.1 vs 68.4, F_env 38.6 vs 22.2.)
    Bearing: predicting changes is not uniformly better. On transitions that do change, full-state prediction matched or
    beat diffs for GPT-4, so B43 has to test the direction rather than assume it.
 e) Li, Nye, Andreas, "Implicit Representations of Meaning in Neural Language Models", ACL 2021, arXiv 2106.00737.
    VERIFIED (abstract): BART/T5 states allow "a linear readout of each entity's current properties and relations"
    (Alchemy, TextWorld); item 1 has the 87.6%-trivial reanalysis. Toshniwal, Wiseman, Livescu, Gimpel, "Chess as a
    Testbed for Language Model State Tracking", AAAI 2022, arXiv 2102.13249. VERIFIED (abstract): with enough data,
    transformers track pieces and predict legal moves; with little data, board-state supervision helps; full attention
    over the history is needed. Both are probing/training studies, not store-vs-LM comparisons.
 f) PROC2PDDL: Tianyi Zhang, Li Zhang, Zhaoyi Hou, Ziyu Wang, Yuling Gu, Peter Clark, Chris Callison-Burch, Niket Tandon.
    NLRSE 2024 workshop. arXiv 2403.00092. VERIFIED: LLMs turn open-domain procedural text into PDDL actions for a
    classical solver. GPT-3.5 success "close to 0%"; GPT-4o 38%, 45% with ZPD instructions vs 34% with CoT (abstract).
    Table 2: GPT-4 action acc 15.9 / problem solve 33.7; GPT-4o 18.2/37.9.
    Bearing (my inference): the solver is exact, so the low success rates come from the LM's translation step. B43 should
    likewise report extraction errors separately from read errors.

Implications
(i) Novelty. I found no paper that compares "LLM extracts per-sentence updates into an external key-value store (VSA or
dict) with deterministic reads" against "the same LLM answers the full grid" on ProPara or on the boxes task, so a hedged
"to our knowledge" is defensible for that exact comparison. "First symbolic/store approach on ProPara" is not: PROPOLIS
(Findings EACL 2023, F1 58.7) scores a symbolic tracker on ProPara with the document-level metric, CoCoGen (EMNLP 2022)
writes ProPara states as code, and IC-DST (Findings EMNLP 2022) shows update extraction plus accumulation beating
full-state prediction for dialogue state (58.7 vs 47.9 JGA). On boxes, prior work is behavioural, mechanistic or about
pretraining data; I found no store or code-execution arm, but my search was not exhaustive.
(ii) CoA / map-reduce on RULER or BABILong. None found. The original CoA paper has NIAH PLUS only in the camera-ready
(text-bison 26.0% -> 97.8% up to 128k; absent from arXiv v1). The one third-party CoA run on synthetic retrieval
(LLMxMapReduce, ACL 2025, InfiniteBench, Llama3-70B) gives PassKey 9.32 / Number 15.59 / KV 1.80, while LongAgent gets
99.32/93.05/74.60 and MapReduce 100/99.79/98.89. BABILong's own baselines are RAG only (RAG-S Llama3 at 128K: QA2 6,
QA3 19). B43 would be among the first CoA results on RULER/BABILong; word it as "we could not find", not "first".
(iii) Cautions. (1) ProPara document-level numbers depend on evaluator version (ProStruct 53.7 in its paper vs 54.5 on
the leaderboard; ProLocal 35.3 vs 50.7): state the evaluator and quote leaderboard-consistent baselines (KOALA/CGLI/MeeT
tables). (2) Use grids.v1 test = 54 paragraphs / 236 participants; IWCS 2025 says 52. (3) Published LLM numbers use other
prompt formats (IWCS per entity and step with the paragraph cut at t; CoCoGen 3-shot code), so they are reference points,
not matched baselines. (4) CoA chains can collapse (CoA camera-ready F.4; MapReduce re-run): use the verbatim prompts,
temperature 0 and 8k chunks, and report the collapse rate, or a store win over CoA can be blamed on a weak
re-implementation. (5) Boxes text is template-generated and password-protected against contamination: do not
redistribute the unzipped data or call it natural text. (6) ProPara label noise (location agreement 0.67; ~11% of
annotations corrected by CRTS) caps every arm's F1. (7) Wang et al. 2024 found diff prediction worse than full-state
prediction on changing transitions, so the store-vs-direct direction is an open question for the prereg, not a given.
