## Manual inspection
- History-engine misses in warehouse and home (48): 30 miss items, 15 add items, 3 both. Misses are moves into the region
  during the window that the reader did not catch (a pallet passing through an aisle) or items it had elsewhere when the
  window opened; extras are transient misreads later corrected. Warehouse is the weakest scenario (0.725), as in
  development: a window accumulates every transient error inside it, while the end state only needs the last write.
- LLM misses (344, normalising parse): 153 missed items, 40 no answer, 38 extra items, 26 equal to the system-of-record
  history (human messages ignored), 14 equal to the final-state answer (window ignored), 73 other. 10 sampled answer lines
  were read: all parse as written; the errors are wrong sets, not format failures.

## Conclusion
Impact questions about a past window cannot be answered from the current state (0.04) and are mostly missed by a strong
open LLM reading the whole log (0.20-0.25). An engine that keeps the read state's history and takes the union over the
window answers 0.87 on fresh logs. Here too the engine's errors are the reader's (moves missed or misread inside the
window); the history engine itself is exact (truth history: 1.000).
Limits: as NSR1 (simulated sites, one open LLM, no frontier long-context model); windows are drawn from the log's own
clock; the history engine is deterministic (no reliability weighting; NSR2 found that weighting does not add for flagging).

## Literature check (conclusion -> literature)
SUPPORTED: LLM accuracy on interval and timeline questions tops out at 36% for frontier models (Test of Time, Table 5,
opened for docs/neurosymbolic_review.md Part III); end-to-end state tracking plateaus near 75% and indirect effects are
missed (ActionReasoningBench, Tables 3-4, re-opened). NO PRECEDENT FOUND for window impact questions over a state read
from multi-channel logs (Part III.5).

**T2-F (history - frontier >= 0.30): FAIL.** Difference +0.044, paired 95% bootstrap interval +0.002 to +0.087. With

The frontier model beats the history engine on warehouse (0.850 vs 0.725; frontier-only right 18 of 80 warehouse
questions). Warehouse is where the reader's transient misreads accumulate inside a window. The engine leads on
biobank (0.933 vs 0.717) and home.
Frontier misses (78): 41 miss items, 16 add items, 8 both, 8 no answer (2 budget, 6 unparseable underscore IDs in home),
4 equal to the system-of-record history, 1 equal to the final state. 8 sampled answer lines were read: each parses as
written; the errors are sets with one item too many or too few, or underscore-formatted names (home).

**Revised conclusion.** "Window questions are mostly missed by an LLM reading the log" holds for the open model
(Qwen3-32B 0.20-0.25), NOT for a frontier reasoning model at this log length (~10k tokens). That model comes within
0.02-0.04 of the history engine. The engine's case at this scale is the same as in NSR1: cost (USD 0.05 and about 40 s
per question against one 1.7B read of each message and microsecond answers), on-device operation, and traceable
answers. The two error sets are again complementary (either right 0.963).

Literature check: PARTIAL CONFLICT with Test of Time (frontier models of 2024 at most 36% on interval questions).
Those were non-reasoning models on synthetic timeline arithmetic. CONSISTENT with TimE (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv
2505.12891; read only in a secondary summary): reasoning models gain clearly on computation-type temporal tasks,
and our windows are explicit clock ranges. Open, NSR5: whether the frontier model holds as the record grows.
Sensitivity (frontier amendment 1, rule 3): the 2 budget-exhausted questions were re-run with a 64,000-token budget.
Both came back correct, one after 41,632 tokens and one after 210 (so its first exhaustion was stochastic). With
them counted, the frontier score is 0.835 and the difference +0.039. T2-F is unchanged.
Correction (2026-11-11, prereg/NSR_frontier_amendment2.md, POST HOC). The 6 "unparseable" frontier answers above were
NOT underscore IDs. In each, the stream reassembly had glued every "Answer:" to the end of a progress note. With the
line break restored (--repair) the frontier model scores 0.839 (0.867 with the normalising parse; micro-F1 0.965 /
0.976). History - frontier: +0.035 (-0.007 to +0.076); with the normalising parse +0.007 (-0.035 to +0.046). T2-F
fails on every parse; the history engine and the frontier model are statistically tied.
