## RULER: every sample on which the Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-8B (B21) dict answers differ (52 of 5,400; all lengths)
All 52 were read chunk by chunk (the differing reader outputs are printed next to each other in the discord file).
- 43 favour the 32B reader:
  - 31: the 8B reader returned "[]" for a chunk that holds a needle (missed extraction); the 32B read it.
  - 2: the 8B miscopied a key or value (e.g. "brany-expansionism" for "brainy-expansionism"; uuid
    "8a0aeef0-1111-..." for "8a0aeef0-1bdd-..."), so the grounding check dropped the pair.
  - 10: VT, the 8B skipped one link of the variable chain ("VAR B = VAR A" lines); the 32B read it.
- 9 favour the 8B reader:
  - 5: the 32B "corrected" the spelling of a key while copying it ("forgoing-glance" for "foregoing-glance" twice,
    "forgoing-transparency", "reminescent-mask" for "reminiscent-mask"), so its pair fails the grounding check.
  - 4: VT, the 32B skipped one chain link (once it wrote "NAVG" for "NAVGC").
- At 128k: 5 differ, all in the 32B reader's favour (2 needles the 8B missed or miscopied, 3 VT links).
Reading: the store and the rules are identical, so every difference is a reading difference of the same kind as the
8B's own residual errors (missed needle, miscopied symbol, missed chain link). The 32B makes fewer of them; it also
introduces one error type the 8B rarely makes (normalising the spelling of a copied symbol).

## Overall
On both benchmarks the residual errors of every reader are reading errors (a missed or miscopied symbol, a wrong
antecedent, a name outside the registry); the store, the dict and the rules never disagree (dict = store / matrix
memory on every question of every reader that has finished). Larger readers trade error types rather than remove
them on the logs; on RULER the 32B removes most of the 8B's few misses.
