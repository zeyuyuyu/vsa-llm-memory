## NSR0b: is the noise win pruning? (T-prune tuned on validation: s = 1, q = 0.5)
B1 (V-delta beats T-prune): PASS in F_p0.1/0.2/0.3_m3 (e.g. F_p0.3_m3 0.542 vs 0.282). B2: FAIL in those cells,
   holds in L_p0.3_m3 (T-prune 0.871 > V-delta 0.780). My prediction (B1 fails everywhere) was wrong for fact noise.
Mechanism: fact noise creates spurious keys with one vote (share 1.0), which a share threshold keeps and a minimum
   support of 2 removes; validation (without fact noise) tied s = 1 and s = 2 and the tie rule picked s = 1.
Manual check (prereg item): in 10 sampled L_p0.3_m3 stories where V-delta is right and T wrong, T's error is AMBIG
   from at least one weakly supported rule in 10/10; a support-pruned table (s = 2, q = 0.6) answers 10/10 (0.992 of
   all 749 such stories). F_p0.3_m3: weak rule in 10/10, pruned table right on 4/10 (0.542 of 502).

## Exploratory upper bound (NSR0b, parameters chosen per cell on test; never quoted as a result)
Best T-prune and best V-delta per cell, both chosen on test (nsr/out/nsr0b_upper_score.log): T-prune >= V-delta in
22 of 27 cells, but V-delta was ahead in the m = 1 noisy cells (L_p0.1/0.2/0.3_m1 +0.151 / +0.145 / +0.100;
F_p0.1/0.2_m1 +0.024 / +0.036). Diagnosis (L_p0.2_m1): plain T learns 176 rules (117 clean) and answers AMBIG 77% of the
time, because a wrong k = 2 rule spawns wrong derived rules in closure; V-delta keeps 121 rules (41% AMBIG). T-prune
uses one threshold inside and after closure, so it cannot keep single-vote k = 2 rules while demanding support for
derived ones. This lead was registered and tested as NSR0d and NSR0e.

## Verification
Independent scorers for every stage (nsr0*_score.py). NSR0's recomputed all 1,420 accuracies from per-story predictions (0 mismatches); a second,
separately written table implementation reproduced T's per-story answers in 4 cells (S_all, S_m1, L_p0.3_m3, F_p0.3_m3;
0 of 1,146 differ in each); the vectorised CYK equals the reference CYK on validation and test (predictions and
confidences). Seeds vary training data, noise and codebooks. Not checked: another dataset (NSR1 is the next angle).

## Conclusion
On relation symbols, a count table learns kinship composition from one story per chain, is 75x smaller than the vector
at D = 2048 and more accurate, and gives confidence at least as useful. Under label or fact noise every VSA store's
apparent advantage traced to how weakly supported rules are handled, and a symbolic table with staged support
thresholds and derivation voting beats every VSA store tested in every cell, confirmed on fresh data (NSR0e). Two
registered bars passed along the way (NSR0 A2, NSR0d D1) and both were explained by a weaker table variant, never by
something the table family cannot do. No NSR claim may attribute reasoning to VSA.
Not tested: VSA with structured, non-commutative codes that could reuse a rule on unseen relation pairs (the one
VSA-specific advantage with published evidence, Learn-VRF); another dataset.
