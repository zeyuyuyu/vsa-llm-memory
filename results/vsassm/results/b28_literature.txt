B28 literature check (after the verdict; prereg "Checks from other angles"). Searched 2026-11-17 ~20:00-20:50 UTC.

Finding: SUPPORTS. The commutativity of HRR binding, the ambiguity it causes, and the remedy B28 measured
(permute one argument before binding) are textbook, stated by the inventors of the two algebras. No novelty can be
claimed for the remedy; B28 only measures what it does on bAbI tasks 4 and 15 and in arena C. No precedent was found
for that measurement on bAbI.

1. Plate, T. A. (1995). Holographic reduced representations. IEEE Trans. Neural Networks 6(3), 623-641
   (doi 10.1109/72.377968). Read in the scanned original (OCR of pp. 9, 11-12):
   - Sec. VIII-G "Noncommutative Variants and Analogs of Convolution": "The commutativity of convolution can cause
     ambiguity in the representations of some structures. If this is a problem, noncommutative variants of
     circular convolution can be computed by permuting the elements of the argument vectors in either the spatial
     or frequency domain. The permutations applied to right and left vectors must be different. The resulting
     operation is neither commutative nor associative, but is bilinear and has an easily computed approximate
     inverse." This is B28's perm encoding (identity on the key, a random permutation on the value).
   - p. 9: commutativity "means that Pr(E|H) cannot be distinguished from Pr(H|E) in the blending clean-up
     memory. This drawback could be overcome by using one of the noncommutative variants of circular convolution
     (Section VIII-G)" -- the same failure as tasks 4 and 15, named by Plate.
   - Sec. VIII-F: convolution by a fixed vector is a circulant matrix. A cyclic shift is a circulant permutation,
     so it commutes with convolution; this is why B28's shift control reproduces comm question for question
     (derived; Plate's "must be different" condition is necessary, not sufficient -- no source found that says
     a cyclic shift fails under HRR, so this point is our derivation, consistent with Sec. VIII-F).
2. Kanerva, P. (2009). Hyperdimensional computing. Cognitive Computation 1, 139-159. "Representing Pairs with
   Vector Multiplication": XOR binding "both commutes ... and is its own inverse ... This can be avoided with a
   slightly different multiplication that neither commutes nor is a self-inverse. As with sequences, we can
   encode the order of the operands by permuting one of them before combining them", C = PA XOR B.
   "Representing Sequences by Permuting Sums": order is labelled with permutations (S = PD + E).
3. Schlegel, Neubert, Protzel (2022). A comparison of vector symbolic architectures. Artif. Intell. Rev.
   (arXiv 2001.11797): HRR circular convolution "is commutative as well as associative"; VTB and MBAT bindings are
   "neither commutative nor associative"; permutations are "important, e.g., to quote information" (citing
   Gayler's MAP) and were deliberately left out of their comparison.
4. Gosmann and Eliasmith (2019). Vector-derived transformation binding. Neural Computation 31(5), 849-869: a
   non-commutative binding proposed as an alternative to HRR (abstract and documentation checked; full text not
   read). Not tested in B28 (needs a square dimension; 2048 is not one).
5. Gayler (2003), Multiply-Add-Permute: permutation as the third MAP operation ("quote"/protect); cited via
   Schlegel et al.; original not re-read.

What this licenses: "standard remedy (Plate 1995, Sec. VIII-G; Kanerva 2009)". What it does not: any claim that
the remedy is ours, or that role vectors in general fail -- B28's rtag control is role vectors BOUND INTO the pair
by convolution, not Plate's additive role-filler frames (agent (*) a + patient (*) b), which were not tested.
