## Why the end state does not move
The reader is already right on 97.5% of final facts (614/630). R1's 8 duplicate swaps are all mid-log: a later event
overwrites both items before the end, so they corrupt the history (419 fact-line states, post hoc R1-only run) but no
final fact. R3 repairs one final fact (biobank-605-5: DS-14 read into box CB-014; truth: handed to the FedEx courier)
and 195 more history states. The impact questions read only the final state, so they cannot see these repairs; the
temporal questions read the whole history and gain.
Post hoc R1-only arm (nsr/out/nsr3_nsr3_nsr4_R1posthoc.jsonl, not registered): temporal +0.015 (7 of E1's 9 fixes).

## Every broken fact inspected
E1: none. E2: biobank-604-4 S-26-01785 (truth CB-003). The reader turned "stuck it in CB-3" (line 190) into two moves,
CB-001 then CB-003; R2 keeps the first place and drops the second, so the fact goes wrong. This is the failure the
prereg named for R2 ("the first stands"); the 81 broken history states are the same mechanism on other lines.

## Predictions (made before the held-out split existed)
S1 about +0.02: WRONG (+0.000; development had final-fact repairs, 9 and 5, that nsr3 does not). S2 about +0.03:
+0.020, holds. S3 and S4 hold: S4 holds; S3 could not be measured (1 repair), with no fact broken. "E2 adds little over E1
and may break facts": half right. E2 adds +0.013 temporal over E1 (CI +0.004 to +0.024) and breaks 1 final fact and 81
history states.

## Conclusion
On fresh logs, symbolic consistency repair of the reader's events makes the temporal answers measurably better (+0.020,
nine fixes, nothing broken) and never breaks a fact in the primary arm. It does not move end-state answers on this split,
because the errors the rules can see are mid-log and later events overwrite them. Unlike NSR2's engine, which could flag
risky answers but never change one, this engine changes answers, and only for the better. The rules cover a small part of
the reader's errors: E1 leaves 39 of 460 temporal questions and 17 of 230 impact questions wrong, and most remaining misses
are plausible wrong places that no consistency rule can see.

## Literature check (conclusion -> literature)
SUPPORTED in kind: global constraint inference over the decisions of local extractors (ILP with symmetry/transitivity
constraints on temporal relations) improves over the local decisions (Do et al. 2012, as cited in Ning, Feng & Roth,
EMNLP 2017, arXiv 1906.04943; read only in the abstract and citation context, gain size not verified). Here the
constraints are domain rules about state (a swap read twice is one swap; type containment) applied to a neural reader's
events, and the gain shows on history questions, not on end-state questions.
