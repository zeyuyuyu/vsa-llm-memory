B33 literature check (prereg "Checks from other angles"). Searched 2026-11-18 ~07:00-07:30 UTC, before the verdict;
the reading of each item against the B33 result is added after the verdict (bottom).

1. Yang, Ishay and Lee (2023). Coupling large language models with logic programming for robust and general
   reasoning from text. Findings of ACL 2023, 5186-5219 (arXiv 2307.07696; read in the ar5iv HTML). GPT-3 as a
   few-shot semantic parser into logical facts + an answer-set-programming solver: 100.0 on bAbI task 2 (two
   supporting facts; few-shot prompting 60.8, chain of thought 72.2), 99.99% averaged over the 20 tasks; the only two
   disagreements with the labels are attributed to malformed questions. -> PRECEDENT for the route's principle (an LM
   reading the text into symbols + an exact symbolic reasoner answers task 2 exactly); no "first" claim is possible.
   B33 differs in the reader (frozen Phi-2 + a naming head trained on identity labels, a hand-written parse), in the
   store (a VSA) and in testing on questions no part of the work had used.
2. Recht, Roelofs, Schmidt and Shankar (2019). Do ImageNet classifiers generalize to ImageNet? ICML 2019 (PMLR 97).
   Test sets rebuilt by following the original collection process cost trained models 3-15 points (CIFAR-10) and
   11-14 points (ImageNet); the authors attribute the drop to the new sets being slightly harder, not to test-set
   reuse. -> CAUTION / design support: a re-created test set can shift difficulty for learned models even when built
   to the original recipe. This is why B33 reads every bar on the generated set AND on the official remainder, and
   registered a shift safeguard for the fine-tune.
3. Tamari, Richardson, et al. (2022). Dyna-bAbI: unlocking bAbI's potential with dynamic synthetic benchmarking.
   *SEM 2022, 101-122 (arXiv 2112.00086). bAbI lacked a controllable generator; with one, models trained on bAbI-style
   data generalised poorly to new compositions (< 70% on complex compositions). -> SUPPORT for regenerating bAbI data
   to test generalisation; also a reason why a learned model, unlike a rule, may lose accuracy on regenerated stories.
4. facebookarchive/bAbI-tasks README (cloned 2026-11-18): "This code is a rewrite of the original code that was used
   to generate the publicly available dataset. As such, it is not possible to produce exactly the same dataset.
   However, we have verified that numbers obtained are very similar." Its WhereIsObject task and templates differ from
   the public task-2 data (see prereg). -> the reason B33 re-implemented the public distribution rather than running
   the released generator.

Reading after the verdict (results/b33_verdict.txt):
- Item 1 SUPPORTS the B33 result and removes any novelty claim for it: an LM-read symbolic route answering task 2
  exactly is published (GPT-3 + ASP, 100.0). What B33 adds is narrower: the paper's own route (frozen Phi-2, naming
  head, VSA, hand-written parse) holds at 1.000 on questions it had never seen, after its parse was corrected on the
  test set it was first scored on -- i.e. the post-hoc 500/500 was not test-set fitting.
- Item 2 predicted a possible drop for learned models on a re-created set; B33 saw none beyond noise for the
  fine-tune (best of twelve 0.964 on fresh vs 0.971 on the 1,000 official test questions; shift flag not raised) and a
  small one for prompting (0.357 vs 0.376-0.386), which the hand-reading partly attributes to the fresh set's larger share of
  dropped-object questions. NO CONFLICT; the two-set design was not needed to rescue any reading (both sets agree).
- Item 3: the fine-tune's errors concentrate where the dropper walks on after the drop (0.85-0.92 there vs 0.97-0.99
  elsewhere), the compositional case that bAbI-trained models are known to find hard; CONSISTENT, no direct precedent
  for this specific breakdown was searched.
- Precedent for "memory ahead of a tuned fine-tune by about 3 points on task 2": none sought or claimed; B33 makes no
  advantage claim (below the registered ten-point bar).
