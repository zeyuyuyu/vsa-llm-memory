# before any B49 memory comparison; every entry's URL was opened by the subagent; claims from abstracts where only the

## Q1. Anisotropy / isotropy of LLM hidden states and sentence embeddings
- Ethayarajh 2019, How Contextual are Contextualized Word Representations?, EMNLP-IJCNLP (D19-1006): BERT, ELMo, GPT-2
  representations anisotropic in every layer.
- Gao et al., Representation Degeneration Problem in Training Natural Language Generation Models, ICLR 2019
  (arXiv 1907.12009): likelihood training with tied embeddings pushes word embeddings into a narrow cone.
- Timkey & van Schijndel 2021, All Bark and No Bite: Rogue Dimensions in Transformer Language Models Obscure
  Representational Quality, EMNLP (2021.emnlp-main.372): 1-3 rogue dimensions dominate cosine; standardisation reveals
  the representational quality.
- Su et al. 2021, Whitening Sentence Representations for Better Semantics and Faster Retrieval (arXiv 2103.15316);
  Huang et al. 2021, WhiteningBERT (arXiv 2104.01767): whitening makes sentence vectors more isotropic and improves STS.
- Cai et al. 2021, Isotropy in the Contextual Embedding Space: Clusters and Manifolds, ICLR: globally anisotropic,
  locally isotropic within clusters / low-dimensional manifolds.
- Rudman et al. 2022, IsoScore, Findings of ACL (arXiv 2108.07344): common isotropy metrics unreliable. Rudman &
  Eickhoff 2024, Stable Anisotropic Regularization, ICLR (arXiv 2305.19358): decreasing isotropy improves fine-tuned
  performance on most tasks. Ding et al. 2022 (Insights workshop, 2022.insights-1.1): isotropy calibration gives no
  consistent gains.
- Razzhigaev et al. 2024, The Shape of Learning, Findings of EACL (arXiv 2311.05928): decoder anisotropy peaks in the
  middle layers. Machina & Mercer 2024, Anisotropy is Not Inherent to Transformers, NAACL (2024.naacl-long.274).
- Valeriani et al. 2023, The geometry of hidden representations of large transformer models, NeurIPS (arXiv 2302.00294):
  intrinsic dimension expands then contracts across layers. Cheng et al. 2025, ICLR (arXiv 2405.15471): high-ID middle
  phase transfers best. Tsukagoshi & Sasano 2025, Findings of ACL (arXiv 2506.01435): retrieval / STS embeddings have
  higher ID and are more isotropic than classification / clustering embeddings.

## Q2. Superposition capacity and cross-talk vs dimension and key correlation
- Kohonen 1972, Correlation Matrix Memories, IEEE Trans. Computers C-21(4):353-359: non-orthogonal keys cause cross-talk.
- Plate 1995, Holographic Reduced Representations, IEEE TNN 6(3):623-641: capacity roughly linear in dimension for
  i.i.d. vectors; similarity among vectors raises the error rate; matrix memories have higher capacity per dimension.
- Frady, Kleyko & Sommer 2018, A theory of sequence indexing and working memory in recurrent neural networks, Neural
  Computation 30(6) (arXiv 1803.00412): cross-talk limits retrieval; a Wiener-filter (statistically optimal) readout
  gives much higher capacity.
- Thomas, Dasgupta & Rosing 2021, Theoretical foundations of hyperdimensional computing, JAIR 72 (arXiv 2010.07426):
  exact decoding of bundles of size <= s needs a 1/(2s)-incoherent codebook; correlated keys lower s.
- Clarkson, Ubaru & Yang, Capacity Analysis of Vector Symbolic Architectures (arXiv 2301.10352; JAIR 86): dimension
  bounds; MAP linked to Johnson-Lindenstrauss sketches and Bloom filters.
- Kleyko et al. 2023, Neural Computation (arXiv 2305.16873): interference-cancelling decoders raise the information
  rate of the same superposition from 0.60 to 1.26 bits per dimension.
- Neubert & Schubert 2021, CVPR (arXiv 2101.07720): mean-centring descriptors before bundling improves
  quasi-orthogonality.
- Gap: no paper found showing that ZCA whitening of keys raises VSA capacity.

## Q3. Pseudo-inverse (least-squares) vs Hebbian associative memory
- Kohonen & Ruohonen 1973, IEEE Trans. Computers C-22(7):701-702: pseudo-inverse recall exact for linearly independent
  keys (pairs <= key dimension), least-squares otherwise.
- Personnaz, Guyon & Dreyfus 1986, Phys. Rev. A 34:4217: projection rule, errorless storage and retrieval.
- Kanter & Sompolinsky 1987, Phys. Rev. A 35:380: any linearly independent set recalled up to alpha = 1, basins shrink
  to 0 as alpha -> 1.
- Loewe 1998, Ann. Appl. Prob. 8(4):1216-1250: Hebbian storage of correlated patterns, capacity falls with correlation.
- Schlag, Irie & Schmidhuber 2021, ICML (arXiv 2102.11174): additive outer-product memory has a capacity limit; the
  delta rule fixes it at the same state size.
- Gap: no equal-bits comparison of a pseudo-inverse matrix memory with a single VSA vector found.

## Q4. Learned (supervised) binary hashing
- Strecha, Bronstein, Bronstein & Fua 2012, LDAHash, TPAMI 34:66-78: projection = whitening by the covariance of
  matched-pair differences, then thresholding; 64-128-bit codes.
- Gong, Lazebnik, Gordo & Perronnin 2013, ITQ / CCA-ITQ, TPAMI 35(12): LSH too noisy at few bits; data-adapted codes
  matter most at small code sizes; CCA-ITQ beats unsupervised variants.
- Liu et al. 2012, Supervised Hashing with Kernels, CVPR: gains from learning from pairs.
- Sablayrolles et al. 2017, ICASSP (arXiv 1609.06753): standard protocols overstate supervised hashing; evaluate on
  disjoint classes (our queries are unseen entities' aliases, a disjoint-class setting).
- Wang et al., A Survey on Learning to Hash, TPAMI (arXiv 1606.00185).
- Gap: all evidence at 32-256 bits per item; nothing at 4-16.

## Q5. Method rankings across embedding models
- Aumueller & Ceccarello 2021, Information Systems 101 (arXiv 1907.07387): local intrinsic dimensionality drives ANN
  difficulty.
- Caspari et al., CoRECT (arXiv 2510.19340, preprint): compression performance varies across models.
- Xiao (arXiv 2605.17524, single-author preprint): covariance structure governs binary-quantisation fidelity across
  embedding models.
- Gap: no comparison of sketches, LSH or VSA stores on LLM hidden states vs sentence encoders found.

## Synthesis before the result
(a) Cross-talk theory (Kohonen, Plate, Thomas et al., Frady et al.) ties superposition capacity to how close the keys
are to orthogonal; more usable dimensions after whitening (larger d') should favour superposition: consistent with HD.
Direct evidence that whitening raises VSA capacity: none found.
(b) Classical theory predicts a pseudo-inverse / least-squares memory beats the Hebbian rule at equal state size,
with the caveat of shrinking basins near capacity (our queries are perturbed keys).
(c) Learned hashing should beat LSH most at short codes, but the evidence is at >= 32 bits and on seen classes.
(d) Raw anisotropy matters mainly through rogue dimensions that distort cosine; whether isotropy helps is contested.

Not verified by the subagent: Plate's 2003 book, Personnaz et al. 1985, the Hebbian alpha ~ 0.138 figure.

## After the result (2026-11-20)
- The least-squares associative memory (LAM) beats the MAP store by 1.9-3.4 points over R1 on all seven encoders, at
  V = 2 by 5-16 points; the MAP store beats LAM at V = 16 on the LLM codes. SUPPORTS the classical result that the
  pseudo-inverse / least-squares rule beats the Hebbian outer-product rule at equal state size (Kohonen & Ruohonen 1973;
  Schlag et al. 2021) and Plate 1995's remark that matrix memories have higher capacity per dimension. Own analysis, no
  precedent found: at V = 2 two independent random value codes agree on about half of the store's coordinates, so the
  MAP read (score difference) uses about half of the stored dimensions, which a single discriminant (LAM) does not waste.
- Learned hashing (shrinkage LDA, then the per-fact index) beats unlearned hashing by 1.8-9.3 points over R1 on every
  encoder, on queries of entities never seen in training. SUPPORTS Gong et al. 2013 (data-adapted codes matter most at
  short code lengths); EXTENDS the evidence to 4-16 bits per item; does not support Sablayrolles et al.'s caution that
  supervised hashing gains vanish on unseen classes (our classes are disjoint entities; the map learns alias variation).
- Isotropising the Qwen3-8B codes did not reduce the store's margin (Delta +4.40 vs BEST, +1.81 vs BEST36) and
  anisotropising e5 did not create one (-1.70 / -0.11). CONFLICTS with the anisotropy account; CONSISTENT with Ding et
  al. 2022 and Rudman & Eickhoff 2024 (isotropy itself gives no consistent gain).
- Capping the Qwen3-8B codes at 768 dimensions kept most of the margin over B36's classical memories (+6.77 -> +5.44;
  Granite +6.06 -> +3.86), far above e5's +0.60 at the same dimension: the LLM / sentence-encoder difference is not the
  ambient dimension. No precedent found (gap).
