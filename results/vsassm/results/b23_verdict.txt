## Reading (written by hand after the generated part; the pre-registered reading rules are in the prereg)
- The end-to-end fine-tuned 1.7B model is a strong baseline. With no memory it reaches 99.2 / 99.2 / 98.2 / 98.0 /
  97.4 / 94.6 at 0k / 4k / 16k / 32k / 64k / 128k (primary arm Y, seed 0). That is far above the predictions (72 at
  128k): the prediction ranges at 64k and 128k did NOT hold.
- 0k-32k: the baseline matches our system. The differences are 0.4-1.4 points, and none is significant at n = 500.
  At 4k the baseline is nominally better (99.2 vs 97.8, p = 0.09; seed 1: 99.6, p = 0.012, i.e. significantly
  better than ours). Up to 32k, then, task fine-tuning alone accounts for the result, as the referee said.
- 64k (the longest training length of arm Y): our system is better by 2.2 points (p = 0.007; seed 1: 2.0, p = 0.006).
  The gap is real but smaller than the 5-point bar, so bar 2 FAILS. Under the pre-registered reading, at 64k the
  fine-tuned model comes within the margin of our system.
- 128k (twice the longest training length): our system is better by 5.2 points (27 questions only ours gets right,
  1 only the baseline; p = 2e-7), so bar 1 PASSES. The margin is fragile: the seed-1 replicate gives 4.8 points
  (p = 1e-7), just under the 5-point bar. The significance holds in both seeds; the size sits at the bar.
- Arm N, trained only inside the native window with YaRN switched on at test, drops further: 94.6 at 64k and 91.2 at
  128k (gaps of 5.0 and 8.6 points, both p < 1e-6).
- Beyond the window: 512k, 1M and 10M inputs exceed the 131k-token YaRN window, so the baseline cannot read them at
  all. Our system answers them (100% at 512k and 1M, n = 125; 10M, n = 25).
- Small versus large at 128k: on the frontier subset (n = 125) the fine-tuned baseline (93.6; arm N 87.2, Y_s1 95.2)
  is below GPT-6.1 Sol (96.8) and Opus 5.5 (97.6), and our system is at 100. At 128k, then, fine-tuning alone does
  not reproduce "small beats large"; at 0k-64k the fine-tuned baseline is level with the large models.
- In one sentence: fine-tuning explains most of the BABILong result up to 64k; the memory's advantage is a 2-point
  gap at 64k, a 5-point gap at 128k, and inputs beyond the window, which only the memory can read.
- Where the baseline errs: mostly qa3 at every length, plus qa4 at 128k (86%, the weakest cell; reversed-direction
  questions). Outputs are clean single labels (2-4 generated tokens, none at the 32-token limit). A few wrong
  answers are book words ("stomach").

## Checks from other angles
- Second scorer: the verbatim official compare_answers agrees on every test row except two, Y_s0 32k "Freddy" for
  "Fred" and Y_s1 64k "Jefferson" for "Jeff". The official substring rule counts these as right; our whole-word rule
  counts them as wrong. So our scorer is the stricter of the two, and the cells shift by at most 0.2.
- Comparison rows: every paired question matched our banked B18 rows on question and target (0 mismatches). Our
  per-length B18 accuracies recomputed from those rows equal the B18 verdict (99.6 / 97.8 / 99.2 / 99.6 / 99.8).
- Read by hand: 10 wrong 128k answers (real wrong rooms or names, no format problem) and 8 random right answers (all
  the exact label).
- Determinism: two 128k qa4 samples were run in both Y_s0 test jobs, which were separate processes on different
  GPUs. Their outputs are identical.
- Seed replicate (Y_s1): within 0.6 points of Y_s0 at every length, and it agrees on every bar direction. It misses
  bar 1 by 0.2 points.
- Literature: the BABILong paper (Kuratov et al. 2024, Sec. 3.3) fine-tuned GPT-3.5 and Mistral-7B on 1,000 QA1
  samples. It reports that fine-tuning "makes scores uniform across all input sizes" within their 16K / 32K
  windows. That agrees with B23 and supports it: an end-to-end fine-tuned transformer closes the gap inside its
  window. B23 adds that a gap opens past the trained length, and that such a model cannot read past its window.

## Deviations and what went wrong (no design change; nothing selected on test results)
- **Preflight bug (fixed before any run finished training; commit 1b01b8b).**
  - The first Y_s0 job (15758342) ran out of memory in its preflight on 66k tokens. from_pretrained returns eval mode,
    and in eval mode gradient checkpointing is skipped.
  - Fix: model.train() is now called before the preflight. Training itself always ran in train mode.
  - Y_s0 and Y_s1 were resubmitted. The first N_s0 job (15758343) was cancelled; it had started with the pre-fix code
    and also sat on the slow node (next point).
- **Slow node atl1-1-03-018-14-0 (H200).**
  - Training there was more than 10x slower. The GPU showed 100% utilisation at about 128 W and 35 C, while the same
    code ran normally on atl1-1-02-012-9-0.
  - Two jobs on that node were cancelled (0.6 GPU-hours). The Y_s1 resubmission excludes the node via scontrol.
- **Y_s0 test split across two jobs.**
  - The run's own job did 0k-64k and 128k qa1-qa3. A parallel job (15769169, same adapter and code) did 128k qa4-qa5.
  - The main job was cancelled after its 128k qa3. The two files were merged by (task, length, index), and the 2 rows
    that overlap were identical.
  - Raw files are in b23_out/parts/.
- **Comparison data:** our system's numbers are the banked B18 per-question results (b23_ref/b18_s0); B18 was not
  rerun. B18 has no 32k run, so 32k has no comparison.
- **Unchanged from the prereg:** the window rule (testlen: longest 128k prompt 129,613 tokens, so factor 4 everywhere
  and arm N native up to 32k), the gate, the primary choice and the scorer.
- **Not used:** the A800 fallback; no PACE job waited more than about 15 minutes.
