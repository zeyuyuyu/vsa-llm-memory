## Reading (written by hand after the generated part; the reading rules are in the prereg)

Part a, query side (primary; the question names the variant; 4,800 RULER needle samples x 5 variant conditions):
- The exact table and B21's random-code store fail completely once the question names the entity differently: 0.0
  in every variant condition, and they agree with each other on all 28,800 sample-conditions. Normalizing case and
  separators (En) recovers 71.6 on format variants and nothing else.
- The VSA store with character-trigram codes (dev choice) restores almost all of the clean accuracy: 99.71 / 99.69 /
  99.59 on the three typo rates, 99.71 on format variants, 98.57 on short aliases, against 99.71 with unchanged names.
  On unchanged names every arm gives the same score as the exact table on all 4,800 samples, so the similarity codes
  cost nothing on clean queries.
- Against the mandatory fair table (normalized edit distance, the dev choice): equal on T1, T2 and P (0 discordant
  samples); edit distance is slightly better on the heaviest typos (T3: -0.12, 6 samples to 0, all niah_multikey_2,
  where trigram overlap prefers a key that shares the adjective, e.g. "damp-apsty" -> "damp-manor"); the VSA is much
  better on short aliases (+6.16, 341 to 2), because edit distance prefers short keys (including the reader's junk keys
  such as "noora") over the long key that contains the alias. Pooled +1.21 [+1.08, +1.34]: bar 2 PASSES, carried
  entirely by aliases.
- Against WRatio: equal on typos and format; better on aliases (+2.35, 178 to 22). Pooled +0.50 [+0.39, +0.61]:
  significant but below the registered +1.0, so bar 3 FAILS.
- V against N (a plain table whose fuzzy index is the exact cosine of character-trigram counts): +0.02 [-0.00, +0.04]
  pooled; V chose the same key as exact trigram cosine on 846 of 849 sampled queries (2 ties). The vector form
  behaves as character-trigram cosine; superposition and clean-up add nothing beyond that similarity.
- Registered reading for bar 2 PASS, bar 3 FAIL: the VSA beats plain edit distance but not, by the registered margin, a
  stronger standard fuzzy matcher; the advantage is the similarity built into its codes, which a table with an n-gram
  index reproduces exactly (N). The paper may not claim that the vector form beats an equally exact table.

Part a, write side (secondary; the text carries the variant, the reader re-reads those chunks, the question is exact):
- E and Ev 0.0 again; V 98.70 (T2), 98.56 (P), 97.01 (A); L 99.05 / 97.18 / 73.73; W 99.03 / 98.16 / 97.92; N equals V
  (+0.02 pooled).
- Here the fair WRatio table is significantly BETTER than the VSA: pooled V - W = -0.28 [-0.43, -0.14] (26 samples to
  57); on aliases -0.91 (6 to 49); 9 of the 10 hand-read cases are niah_multikey_2 stores where WRatio is right: when the stored key is the
  short alias ("drug"), trigram cosine prefers keys that share the question's long adjective ("outstanding-landmine")

  as on the query side, but the sign of V - W flips: which fuzzy measure wins depends on where the variation sits, not
  on the vector form.

Part b (PCM device model, 4,776 probed keys, 16 pairs per vector):
- At the measured device parameters (x1) every arm reads every key at every time to 1 year (H0 PASS): no bit errors.
- With every noise source scaled x4 and beyond, the unprotected table fails first (30 days: 79.3% at x4, 23.1% at x8),
  while the VSA store holds (100% at x4, 98.6% at x8): H2 PASSES (+75.5 points at x8, 30 d).
- The same facts in a table that spends the VSA store's cell count on a plain repetition code (39-46 copies per bit
  on the large stores, up to 227 on the small ones; majority vote) are more robust than the VSA store at every level where either fails: 100% vs
  98.6% at x8, 98.8% vs 0.1% at x16 and 59.5% vs 0.0% at x32 (30 days); H1 FAILS. At x16 and x32 even the unprotected
  table reads more keys than the VSA store (3.4% vs 0.1%; 1.0% vs 0.0% at 30 days), because the store's similarity
  threshold fails all at once.
- So the store is robust because it is redundant (about 512 cells per pair on the large stores), and the vector form is a poor code for
  that redundancy: at a fixed cell budget a table with the simplest error-correcting code is more robust.

In one sentence: similarity-preserving codes let the VSA store absorb misspelled, reformatted and abbreviated entity
names that defeat the exact store (0% -> 97-99.7%), but a table with a standard fuzzy index does as well (trigram
cosine: identical; WRatio: within half a point either way), and at equal cell count a repetition-coded table is more
robust on the device model, so B26 does not show that the vector form beats an equally exact table.

## Literature (searched after the result)
- Cohen, Ravikumar & Fienberg 2003 (IJCAI-03 Workshop on Information Integration, 73-78), a comparison of string
  distance metrics for name matching: no single metric dominates; the best was a hybrid of token TF-IDF cosine and
  Jaro-Winkler. SUPPORTED (direction): here too the winner among trigram cosine, edit distance and WRatio changes with
  the kind and place of the variation.
- Joshi, Halseth & Kanerva 2016 (Quantum Interaction, LNCS 10106, 265-274): letter-trigram hypervectors bundled into
  language profiles, compared by inner product. SUPPORTED (same mechanism): a bundle of n-gram codes is a random
  projection of the n-gram count vector, so its inner products track n-gram-count cosine up to O(1/sqrt(d)); V = N here
  is what that predicts.
- Kim, Imani, Moshiri & Rosing 2020 (GenieHD, DATE 2020): hyperdimensional encoding for DNA pattern matching, with the
  gain reported as speed and energy on hardware against an FPGA design, not as more accurate matches. SUPPORTED
  (direction): what the vector form offers is a hardware-friendly way to compute a standard similarity.
- Part b: no study was found that compares a VSA store with a repetition-coded table on the same device model; the
  result is what elementary coding arguments predict (NO PRECEDENT FOUND for the specific comparison).
