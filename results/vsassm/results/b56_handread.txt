## Notes (by me, after reading the 40 queries above)

- Both cells are V = 2 at 4 bits per fact, where both memories sit far above chance only on part of the queries
  (qwen8 cell: VSAS about 66%, LAM about 71%); the two "only one right" sets are large (462 / 682 and 512 / 911
  queries), so a share of every list is coin-flip luck on aliases that carry little of the label's code (qwen8:
  'Italian' -> 'Symphony No. 4', 'Pai' -> 'Pathit Pisitkul', 'Edu' -> 'Eduard Campabadal', 'Josh' -> 'Josh
  Mankiewicz'; e5: 'Neil Timothy Helson' -> 'Trace Cyrus', 'Banwari' -> 'Krishna', 'River City' -> 'Sacramento').
- What separates the lists is not a kind of alias. Each memory's wins include close variants of the label: VSAS
  'Argentine Republic' -> 'Argentina', 'Cadmos' -> 'Cadmus', 'ACM' -> 'A.C. Milan', 'Slave' -> 'The Slave'; LAM
  'Rio de Janeiro' (accent), 'State of Vermont', 'Kirgizia' -> 'Kyrgyzstan', 'co. Dublin', 'William Martin "Billy"
  Joel', 'Francesco Garde' -> 'Francesco Guardi', "L'assassino" -> 'The Assassin'. Counting close variants in the
  e5 lists gives about 6 of LAM's 10 and 5 of VSAS's 10 (qwen8: about 7 and 7): no pattern. The gap
  (911 vs 512 queries in the e5 cell) is a matter of how often, not of which kind of alias; 10 + 10 queries cannot
  say more.
- No sign of a scoring or parsing artifact: gold values, predictions and aliases are consistent with the stored labels;
  the same entity appears in different stores with different values (e.g. Johann Heinrich Roos), as the design draws
  values per store.
