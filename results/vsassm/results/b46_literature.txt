# B46 literature check (written 2026-11-19, before the B46 verdict)
# Purpose: does the B36 result (VSA store beats the best key-free classical memory and the per-fact index at equal bits
# on frozen Qwen3-8B layer-9 name codes; tie on e5-base-v2) have reasons in print to generalize, or not, to a second LLM
# family (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Granite-3.3-8B-Instruct; layer in {1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 4/4} of
# depth and pooling in {mean over name tokens, PromptEOL last token} chosen on dev)?
# Method: every entry was opened on its own page: arXiv metadata (export.arxiv.org API: title, authors, comment/journal
# field, abstract) and, unless marked "(abstract only)", the paper PDF read with pdftotext (copies in the scratchpad,
# lit/b46/pdf/). Venues come from the arXiv comment/journal field, the PDF header or the ACL Anthology record.
# Quotes are verbatim ("..." = elision; dashes and +/- rendered in ASCII). Nothing already in
# results/b36_literature.txt is repeated.
# Tags (relative to "does the VSA-store advantage on LLM hidden-state codes generalize across LLM families?"):
#   SUPPORT = a reason to expect replication; CONFLICT = a reason to expect it may fail or shrink (often "caution":
#   a family-specific property that could break it); CONTEXT = background/method; GAP = shows what has not been done.

== 1. Intermediate layers and pooling as entity/text codes; how the good choice varies across families ==
[SAZ+25] Skean, O., Arefin, M. R., Zhao, D., Patel, N., Naghiyev, J., LeCun, Y., Shwartz-Ziv, R. Layer by layer:
  uncovering hidden representations in language models. ICML 2025 (arXiv:2502.02013v2). Text: on 32 MTEB tasks
  "intermediate layers often surpass the final layer by up to 16% in downstream accuracy"; "The absolute improvement
  ranges from 2% to as high as 16% on average, and the best layer often resides around the mid-depth of the network. This
  phenomena is consistent across all the different architectures." Main models: Pythia-410M (scaled 14M-1B), Mamba-370M,
  BERT-base; Llama3-8B and Qwen 2.5 appear only in side analyses (fine-tuning, math variant).
  -> SUPPORT (weak): a non-final layer beating the last is the norm across architectures, so dev picking a mid layer in a
     second family is expected. CONTEXT: no comparison among 7-8B LLM families; the decoder "sweet spot" rests on Pythia.
[JHL+23] Jiang, T., Huang, S., Luan, Z., Wang, D., Zhuang, F. Scaling sentence embeddings with large language models.
  arXiv:2307.16645 (2023). Text: template 'This sentence: "[text]" means in one word: "'; "We then use last token hidden
  state as its sentence embedding"; compared with "averaging or using the last token" on OPT 125M-66B (STS-B dev,
  Fig. 1) the prompt-based method is better at every size; LLaMA 7B-65B also run.
  -> CONTEXT / METHOD SOURCE for B46's "eol" pooling. Evidence is final-layer, sentence-level, OPT/LLaMA-1; nothing on
     names or intermediate layers.
[LWZ+24] Lei, Y., Wu, D., Zhou, T., Shen, T., Cao, Y., Tao, C., Yates, A. Meta-task prompting elicits embeddings from
  large language models (MetaEOL). ACL 2024 (arXiv:2402.18458v2). Text (Sec. 5.5, LLAMA2 and Mistral-7B, PromptEOL and
  MetaEOL): "the last layer is not always the most effective for STS tasks"; "the third-to-last layers (indexed as -3)
  across all four configurations perform similarly well"; "PromptEOL tends to show more variability in performance
  across different layers compared to MetaEOL"; larger models use -3 of 32, -4 of 40, -8 of 80 ("10% from the final
  layer").
  -> SUPPORT for the eol arm across families: the best PromptEOL layer sat at the same relative depth (~90%) in
     Llama-2-7B and Mistral-7B. CONTEXT: for eol the optimum is late, between B46's 3/4 and 4/4 candidates, not mid-depth.
[BAM+24] BehnamGhader, P., Adlakha, V., Mosbach, M., Bahdanau, D., Chapados, N., Reddy, S. LLM2Vec: large language
  models are secretly powerful text encoders. COLM 2024 (arXiv:2404.05961v2). Text: S-LLaMA-1.3B, LLaMA-2-7B(-chat),
  Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, Meta-LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct; "The dominant paradigm of applying the EOS pooling for models with
  causal attention is outperformed by (weighted) mean pooling"; "Enabling bidirectional attention without any training
  harms performance for S-LLaMA-1.3B and LLaMA-2-7B. ... the performance of Mistral-7B improves"; "we speculate that
  Mistral models are pre-trained with some form bidirectional attention".
  -> SUPPORT for the "mean" arm (mean beat EOS in all families for causal models); CONFLICT (caution): the same
     manipulation moved Mistral's representations in the opposite direction from Llama's, i.e. family-specific
     pre-training leaves a mark on hidden states that a recipe tuned on one family can miss.
[SKF+25] Springer, J. M., Kotha, S., Fried, D., Neubig, G., Raghunathan, A. Repetition improves language model
  embeddings. ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2402.15449v2). Text (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1): "last-token pooling does not work well in
  a zero-shot manner on real data"; "Mean-token pooling is better suited to the zero-shot setting", but under causal
  attention "mean-token embeddings ... exaggerate early token similarity".
  -> SUPPORT for the zero-shot "mean" arm; CONTEXT: the early-token bias is limited for B46 because the shared prefix
     "Name:" is excluded from pooling (name tokens found by character offsets).
[TY26] Tang, Y., Yang, Y. KV-Embedding: training-free text embedding via internal KV re-routing in decoder-only LLMs.
  arXiv:2601.01046v1 (2026; an ACL 2026 long paper with this title is listed in the ACL Anthology, not opened). Text:
  "We observe that ID trajectories vary by architecture"; Qwen3-4B and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 "exhibit clear ID
  minima in middle layers" (layers 12-21 and 13-19); "Llama-3.1-8B shows a more complex trajectory with multiple local
  minima". Table 1 (MTEB average, training-free baselines, final layer) for Qwen3-4B / Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 /
  Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: PromptEOL 0.4478 / 0.4537 / 0.4702; mean pooling 0.3331 / 0.3775 / 0.3507; last token
  0.2721 / 0.3245 / 0.3073.
  -> SUPPORT: the ranking and spread of training-free readouts (PromptEOL > mean > last token) were the same in a Qwen3,
     a Mistral and a Llama-3.1 model. CONFLICT (caution): per-layer geometry differs by family, Llama-3.1-8B's least
     regular, so a depth fraction chosen on Qwen need not be best elsewhere; B46's dev selection is the right guard.
[LLW+26] Lin, A., Li, Z., Wang, Y., Mao, K., Funakoshi, K., Okumura, M. Steering beyond language: improving
  multilingual training-free text embeddings via paired-residual subspace removal. arXiv:2610.23807v1 (2026). Text
  (PromptEOL last token; Llama-2-7b-hf, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Qwen3-8B): "LLaMA-2-7B and Mistral-7B generally benefit from
  extending removal from the final layers toward intermediate layers, with the best starting layers at 17 and 14,
  respectively ... In contrast, Qwen3-8B achieves its highest score when starting at layer 33, with pronounced
  fluctuations at earlier starting layers."
  -> CONFLICT (caution): direct evidence that Qwen3-8B's depth profile is unlike Llama/Mistral's under a layer-wise
     intervention. Qwen3-8B's layer-9 result does not by itself say where (or whether) another family's good layer is.
[KORS25] Kaplan, G., Oren, M., Reif, Y., Schwartz, R. From tokens to words: on the inner lexicon of LLMs. ICLR 2025
  (arXiv:2410.05864v4). Text: "sub-word sequences are combined into coherent whole-word representations at their last
  token"; "this process primarily takes place within the early and middle layers"; robust to "arbitrary splits ...,
  typos"; App. C (Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B, Yi-6B): "the retrieval rate increases over the first several layers,
  peaks around the middle layers, and then decreases in the later layers. The main difference across models lies in the
  peak performance ... more advanced models such as Llama3-8B and Mistral-7B demonstrate superior performance";
  "retrieval generally peaking around layers 15-20".
  -> SUPPORT: word/name identity is assembled at mid-depth the same way in Llama-3 and Mistral, and survives typos (an
     alias-like perturbation). CONTEXT: the peak (layers 15-20 of 32) is nearer B46's 1/2 candidate than its 1/4 one,
     and it sits on the LAST name token, while "mean" also averages earlier sub-word states.
[FAWB24] Feucht, S., Atkinson, D., Wallace, B., Bau, D. Token erasure as a footprint of implicit vocabulary items in
  LLMs. EMNLP 2024, pp. 9727-9739 (ACL Anthology 2024.emnlp-main.543; arXiv:2406.20086v3). Abstract: "last token
  representations of named entities and multi-token words exhibit a pronounced 'erasure' effect, where information about
  previous and current tokens is rapidly forgotten in early layers" (Llama-2-7b, Llama-3-8B). (abstract only)
  -> CONTEXT for mean vs last-token codes: in Llama models the last name token turns into an entity-level code early;
     a mean over name tokens keeps spelling-level information, which helps aliases that share sub-words with the label
     and hurts those that do not. Which pooling dev picks may therefore differ by tokenizer/family.
[GT24] Gurnee, W., Tegmark, M. Language models represent space and time. ICLR 2024 (arXiv:2310.02207v3). Text:
  activations "on the last entity token for each layer"; linear features "smoothly increase in quality throughout the
  first half of the layers of the model before reaching a plateau"; "The gap between the Llama and Pythia models is
  especially striking, and we suspect is due to the large difference in pre-training corpus size (2T and 300B tokens
  respectively)".
  -> SUPPORT: entity-name information is available from the first half of depth onward in a second family (Llama-2).
     CONFLICT (caution): the amount of entity information differed strongly between families and tracked pre-training
     data, so how well rare aliases are encoded (and hence the alias-label code similarity) can move by family.
[LLGT25] Lad, V., Lee, J. H., Gurnee, W., Tegmark, M. The remarkable robustness of LLMs: stages of inference?
  arXiv:2406.19384v3 (2025). Abstract: models "retain 72-95% of their original top-1 prediction accuracy" after layer
  deletion/swaps; "four stages of inference, observed across diverse model families and sizes: (1) detokenization ...
  (2) feature engineering, where task- and entity-specific features are iteratively refined"; text: Pythia, GPT-2,
  Qwen 2.5, LLaMA 3.2 and Phi families, 124M-6.9B.
  -> SUPPORT: an early-to-middle entity-feature stage is shared across families including Qwen and Llama, by relative
     depth. CONTEXT: models <= 6.9B, no Mistral/Granite, behavioural evidence.

== 2. Geometry that differs across families and matters for random-projection / 1-bit superposition codes ==
[TvS21] Timkey, W., van Schijndel, M. All bark and no bite: rogue dimensions in transformer language models obscure
  representational quality. EMNLP 2021 (arXiv:2109.04404). Text: "a small number of rogue dimensions, often just 1-3,
  dominate these measures"; share of total expected cosine contributed by the single top dimension in the most
  anisotropic layers (Table 1): XLNet layer 11 0.996, BERT layer 11 0.884, GPT-2 layer 12 0.763, RoBERTa layer 12
  0.663; "simple postprocessing techniques such as standardization are
  able to correct for rogue dimensions".
  -> CONFLICT (caution): whether one coordinate dominates cosine is model-specific, and random-hyperplane sign bits
     follow the dominant directions, so a 1-bit code can be fine on one family and degenerate on another. Centring
     removes constant offsets only; per-dimension standardization (or B46's ZCA option) is the documented fix.
[SCKL24] Sun, M., Chen, X., Kolter, J. Z., Liu, Z. Massive activations in large language models. COLM 2024
  (arXiv:2402.17762v2). Text: LLaMA2-7B: "two fixed feature dimensions (1415, 2533), and two types of tokens - the
  starting token, and the first period (.) or newline token (\n)"; categories "a) Starting token only. Models include
  LLaMA2-13B, MPT and GPT-2" ... "c) Starting token, delimiter tokens (such as ".", "\n", "'" or ","), and certain word
  tokens with weak semantics (such as "and", "from", "of" or "2") Models include LLaMA2-70B, Mistral-7B, Mixtral-8x7B,
  Falcon-40B and Phi-2"; in LLaMA2-7B they "first appear in layer 2 and remain nearly constant values until layer 30";
  Mistral-7B and Mixtral share dimension 2070; LLaMA3-8B shown in App. A.1; "their values largely stay constant
  regardless of the input". Qwen and Granite not studied; the Mistral checkpoint studied is not v0.3.
  -> CONFLICT (caution), the most concrete family-specific risk found: under mean pooling, a Mistral name containing
     "of", "and", a comma, an apostrophe or a digit would carry a spike thousands of times the median in one coordinate,
     from early layers on. Centring on the training mean does not remove a spike that only some names have, and sign
     bits of random projections would group those names together. (For eol the final token is the same for every name,
     so a spike there is a constant offset that centring removes, but such a position would carry little input
     information.) Worth checking per-dimension max |value| of the Mistral codes before reading a null.
[XTCHL24] Xiao, G., Tian, Y., Chen, B., Han, S., Lewis, M. Efficient streaming language models with attention sinks.
  ICLR 2024 (arXiv:2309.17453v4). Abstract: "the emergence of attention sink is due to the strong attention scores
  towards initial tokens as a 'sink' even if they are not semantically important" (Llama-2, MPT, Falcon, Pythia).
  (abstract only) -> CONTEXT: see [GPD+25].
[GPD+25] Gu, X., Pang, T., Du, C., Liu, Q., Zhang, F., Du, C., Wang, Y., Lin, M. When attention sink emerges in
  language models: an empirical view. ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2410.10781v2). Text: sinks at "specific token positions, e.g. the
  first token (not necessary to be a BOS token)"; "If there are no BOS tokens in the pre-training, the EOS token is
  considered as the BOS"; Table 1 Sink metric on natural text: Mistral-7B 97.49%, LLaMA2-7B 92.47%, LLaMA3-8B 99.02%,
  GPT2-XL 77.00%; chat models comparable to base.
  -> CONTEXT (design check B46 already passes): position 0 is a sink in every family, with or without BOS, so it must
     stay out of pooled tokens; B46 prepends BOS for Llama/Mistral as trained and pools only name tokens.
[RMG+24] Razzhigaev, A., Mikhalchuk, M., Goncharova, E., Oseledets, I., Dimitrov, D., Kuznetsov, A. The shape of
  learning: anisotropy and intrinsic dimensions in transformer-based models. Findings of EACL 2024, pp. 868-874
  (arXiv:2311.05928v2). Abstract: "the anisotropy profile in transformer decoders exhibits a distinct bell-shaped curve,
  with the highest anisotropy concentrations in the middle layers"; text: decoders OPT 125M-13B, Llama-2 7B-13B and
  7B-chat, GPT-2, GPT-J, Falcon-7B, Bloom, Pythia-2.8B, TinyLlama-1.1B, "among all examined models".
  -> CONTEXT/caution: the mid-depth layers the entity literature favours are the most anisotropic in every decoder
     family examined; un-centred cosines and sign bits there are dominated by a common direction. B46 centres codes on
     the training mean, the standard counter.
[MM24] Machina, A., Mercer, R. E. Anisotropy is not inherent to transformers. NAACL 2024, pp. 4892-4907 (ACL Anthology
  2024.naacl-long.274). Text: "we identify a set of Transformer models with isotropic embedding spaces, the large Pythia
  models"; "the large Pythia models optimize their final Layer Norm for isotropy".
  -> CONTEXT: (an)isotropy is a property of the family and its training, not of the architecture, so code geometry
     can differ qualitatively between families of the same layout.
[MGBB25] Macocco, I., Graichen, N., Boleda, G., Baroni, M. Not a nuisance but a useful heuristic: outlier dimensions
  favor frequent tokens in language models. BlackboxNLP 2025 (arXiv:2503.21718v4). Text: last-layer outlier dimensions
  (median |activation| in the top 1%), "between 4 and 36" per model (layers of 3584-5120 dims); "a group of models with
  the same pattern of behavior across the board (pythia-12b, mistral-7b, llama-8b, olmo2-13b, qwen-14b) and three
  models that often deviate in different ways (opt-13b, gemma-9b, stable-12b)"; "we don't find any massive activation
  on the last layer".
  -> CONTEXT for the 4/4-depth candidates: last-layer codes of Llama-3, Mistral and Qwen alike carry a few input-general,
     frequency-tied outlier dimensions (mostly an offset that centring removes; residual variance needs
     standardization/ZCA). Mistral, Llama and Qwen behaved alike here.
[FSL24] Forooghi, A., Sadeghi, S., Lu, J. Whitening not recommended for classification tasks in LLMs. RepL4NLP 2024
  (ACL Anthology 2024.repl4nlp-1.21; arXiv:2407.12886v1). Abstract: "the efficacy of whitening is model-dependent and
  task-dependent. In particular, whitening degenerates embeddings for classification tasks"; text: BERT, SBERT, SimCSE,
  AnglE-LLaMA, LLaMA, LLaMA2, ChatGPT embeddings; PCA, ZCA, PCA-Cor, ZCA-Cor, Cholesky whitening.
  -> CONTEXT: B46's centre+ZCA option is not guaranteed to help every family; it has to be chosen on dev per family
     (as the B36 protocol does), not carried over from Qwen3.

== 3. Cross-model representational similarity: does alias-label structure transfer across families? ==
[HCWI24] Huh, M., Cheung, B., Wang, T., Isola, P. The platonic representation hypothesis. ICML 2024, PMLR 235
  (arXiv:2405.07987v5). Text: alignment by "mutual nearest-neighbor" on Wikipedia captions; LLMs include bloom, olmo,
  openllama, llama, llama3-8b, gemma, mistral-7b, mixtral-8x7b; for each model they "average pool each layer"; "more
  capable language models align better with more capable vision models".
  -> SUPPORT (indirect): text codes of different LLM families share neighbourhood structure, more so with capability.
     CONTEXT: cross-modal and coarse (see [KZGE26]); alias-level structure not tested.
[WS25] Wolfram, C., Schein, A. Layers at similar depths generate similar activations across LLM architectures. COLM
  2025 (arXiv:2504.08775v3). Text: 24 open-weight models incl. Llama 3.1 (8B) and Llama 3.1 IT (8B), Mistral v0.3 (7B),
  Gemma, Phi-3, Falcon (no Qwen, no Granite); last-token activations, mutual k-NN (k = 10) on 2,048 OpenWebText texts;
  "layers at proportionally similar depths generate more similar activations to one another than other pairs of
  layers"; abstract: neighbour relations "tend to vary from layer to layer within a model" and "are approximately shared
  between corresponding layers of different models".
  -> SUPPORT for indexing B46's candidates by relative depth and for expecting Qwen3-8B's layer-9 neighbourhoods to have
     counterparts near 1/4 depth in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B. CONTEXT: k = 10 neighbourhoods of generic
     text, not alias-label pairs; within a model, neighbourhoods change from layer to layer.
[CDK+25] Cheng, E., Doimo, D., Kervadec, C., Macocco, I., Yu, J., Laio, A., Baroni, M. Emergence of a high-dimensional
  abstraction phase in language transformers. ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2405.15471v4). Text: OPT-6.7B, Llama-3-8B, Pythia-6.9B,
  OLMo-7B, Mistral-7B, last-token representations; "All models clearly go through a phase of high intrinsic
  dimensionality that tends to take place relatively early (starting approximately at layer 6 or 7, and mostly being over
  by layer 20), except for OPT, where it approximately lasts from layer 17 to layer 23"; abstract: in this phase
  representations "(2) are the first to viably transfer to downstream tasks; (3) predict each other across different
  LMs"; text: "between models, representations at the peak have close neighborhood structures" (App. H: "broadly
  comparable patterns emerging from CKA").
  -> SUPPORT, the closest result to B46's layer question: for 32-layer Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B the transferable,
     cross-model-shared phase spans ~layers 6-20, which contains B46's 1/4 (8) and 1/2 (16) candidates; Qwen3-8B
     layer 9 of 36 sits at the same relative point. Caution: OPT's phase is late, so a family can be shifted.
[KBGL23] Klabunde, M., Ben Amor, M., Granitzer, M., Lemmerich, F. Towards measuring representational similarity of
  large language models. UniReps workshop @ NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2312.02730v1). Text: last-layer, last-token
  representations of ~7B models (Bloom, Falcon, Galactica, GPT-J, LLaMA, MPT, OpenLLaMA, OPT, Pythia, StableLM ...);
  "some LLMs are substantially different from others" (Falcon and StableLM Alpha on Winogrande, OpenLlama and OPT on
  HumanEval); "challenges of using representational similarity measures that suggest the need of careful study of
  similarity scores to avoid false conclusions".
  -> CONFLICT (caution): cross-family similarity is not uniform; some families are outliers, and measures disagree.
     A second family could lack the Qwen3 pattern for geometric reasons alone.
[UC26] Usama, M., Chang, D. E. Convergence without understanding: when language models agree on representations but
  disagree on reasoning. arXiv:2605.23315v1 (2026). Abstract: 16 instruction-tuned models from 8 families (1.5B-72B;
  Qwen-2.5, LLaMA-3.x, Gemma, Phi ...), "pre-decision representations align (CKA = 0.875)"; text: "Randomly initialized
  (untrained) models exhibit higher pairwise CKA (0.864 +/- 0.007)" than trained ones (0.612).
  -> CONTEXT: CKA-type cross-family similarity is confounded (untrained nets score higher), so "the families look
     alike" is no reason to expect the VSA result to transfer; only a direct replication answers it.
[KZGE26] Koepke, A. S., Zverev, D., Ginosar, S., Efros, A. A. Back into Plato's cave: examining cross-modal
  representational convergence at scale. arXiv:2604.18572v3 (2026). Abstract: "The mutual k-nearest-neighbor metric used
  on 1024 text-image pairs in the original study captures only coarse structure. To keep the alignment from collapsing
  as one scales up the data, k has to grow proportionally"; "neither stronger language models nor richer captions yield
  fine-grained alignment". (abstract only)
  -> CONFLICT (caution) with reading [HCWI24]/[WS25] as evidence that fine, item-level structure (which alias lies
     nearest which of thousands of labels) transfers: what is shared is coarse. The metric critique also applies to
     [WS25]'s k = 10 on 2,048 texts.
[JZSM25] Jha, R., Zhang, C., Shmatikov, V., Morris, J. X. Harnessing the universal geometry of embeddings (vec2vec).
  NeurIPS 2025 (arXiv:2505.12540v4). Abstract: translates "text embeddings from one vector space to another without any
  paired data"; "high cosine similarity across model pairs with different architectures"; text: encoders gtr, clip,
  e5, gte, stella, granite (RoBERTa-based), qwen (Qwen3, one pair); "cosine similarity as high as 0.96".
  -> SUPPORT (indirect): sentence-encoder spaces, e5 among them, share enough geometry to be aligned without pairs,
     consistent with B36's e5 and Qwen3-8B codes reaching nearly the same alias-label criterion (0.698 vs 0.704). Not
     about LLM hidden states or memories; shared geometry did not make the VSA margin transfer from Qwen3-8B to e5 in B36.

== 4. VSA/HDC/associative memories on LLM hidden states of more than one family; cross-encoder memory comparisons ==
[BNLSP26] Bronzini, M., Nicolini, C., Lepri, B., Staiano, J., Passerini, A. Hyperdimensional probe: decoding LLM
  representations via vector symbolic architectures. TMLR 2026 (arXiv:2509.25045v4). Text: MAP-Bipolar codebooks; a
  trained "shallow neural encoder" maps "the embeddings ... of the last token (":") in the latter half" of the layers into
  VSA space; LLMs GPT-2-medium, Pythia-1.4b, Llama 3.1-8B, Phi-4, OLMo-2-32B, Llama 4 Scout; Table 1 "Key | Target"
  60.0% (GPT-2), 66.9 (Pythia), 85.4 (Llama 3.1), 84.8 (Phi-4), 80.1 (OLMo-2), 79.0 (Llama 4), mean 76.0 +/- 9.4;
  "average probing@1 across all models equal to 83%".
  -> GAP/CONTEXT: the only work found that applies a VSA to hidden states of several LLM families. It is a trained probe
     (no superposed store, no bit budget, no classical comparison), reads late layers' last token, and its results vary
     by model (60-85%), the larger modern families ahead. No Qwen, Mistral or Granite.
[DH26] Dalvi, A., Honavar, V. Hyperdimensional cross-modal alignment of frozen language and image models for efficient
  image captioning (HDFLIM). arXiv:2602.23588v1 (2026). Text: angular LSH "r = LSHQ(x) = sign(Qx)" into beta = 50,000
  bipolar hypervectors, binding and bundling into an associative memory built "in a single pass over the data"; frozen
  "Qwen3-4B-Base" hidden states plus DINOv3.txt; captioning "comparable to end-to-end vision-language training methods".
  -> GAP/CONTEXT: a bundled HDC memory over random-projection sign codes of frozen Qwen3 hidden states, the same coding
     family as B36, works for a task; but one LLM family (Qwen3) only, no other reader, no equal-bits comparison with
     sketches, filters or tables.
  No 2025-2026 equal-bits comparison of HDC/VSA with classical structures (Bloom, count-min, LSH, PQ, static functions)
  on neural embeddings was found beyond those already in b36_literature.txt (searches below).

== 5. Entity alias / name-variant robustness across models ==
[NSK+26] Nishida, Y., Shikoda, N., Kishinami, Y., Fujii, R., Morishita, M., Kamigaito, H., Watanabe, T. Revisiting
  non-verbatim memorization in large language models: the role of entity surface forms. ACL 2026 Main
  (arXiv:2604.21882v1). Abstract: RedirectQA uses "Wikipedia redirect information to associate Wikidata factual triples
  with categorized surface forms"; "Across 13 LLMs ... prediction outcomes often change when only the entity surface form
  changes"; "models are more robust to minor orthographic variations than to larger lexical variations such as aliases
  and abbreviations"; "entity frequency often contributes beyond surface frequency". (abstract only)
  -> CONFLICT (caution): alias robustness differs across LLMs and is weakest for the lexical aliases B46 queries with.
     The alias-label code similarity, and with it the absolute accuracies, should move by family; the VSA-vs-classical
     margin depends on that similarity (B36: median alias-label cosine 0.43-0.51), so it need not carry over unchanged.
[SKGG25] Shachar, O., Katz, U., Goldberg, Y., Glickman, O. NER Retriever: zero-shot named entity retrieval with
  type-aware embeddings. Findings of EMNLP 2025 (arXiv:2509.04011v1). Abstract: "internal representations, specifically
  the value vectors from mid-layer transformer blocks, encode fine-grained type information more effectively than
  commonly used top-layer embeddings". (abstract only)
  -> SUPPORT (one family; which LLM not checked): mid-layer internals beat top-layer embeddings as zero-shot entity codes.

== Summary ==
SUPPORT: name/word identity forms at early-to-mid depth alike in Llama-2/3, Mistral, Yi [KORS25, GT24, LLGT25]; the
transferable, cross-model-shared phase sits at matching relative depth (~layers 6-20 of 32 in Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B)
[CDK+25, WS25]; training-free readouts rank the same (PromptEOL > mean > last) on Qwen3, Mistral, Llama-3.1 [TY26].
CONFLICT: depth profiles differ (Qwen3-8B atypical [LLW+26]); Mistral spikes on "of"/"and"/delimiters that centring
cannot remove [SCKL24]; outlier dims, anisotropy, whitening are model-specific [TvS21, RMG+24, MGBB25, FSL24]; shared
structure is coarse [KBGL23, KZGE26, UC26]; alias robustness varies over 13 LLMs [NSK+26]. So usable codes should
replicate, but nothing predicts the SIZE of the VSA margin (cf. B36's e5 tie); P1's mean-at-1/4 is not favoured over
late-layer PromptEOL [LWZ+24]. GAP: no prior cross-family VSA/HDC store or equal-bits comparison (nearest [BNLSP26, DH26]).

== After the result (2026-11-19, 09:45 box clock): support / conflict / gap ==
Result: on Granite-3.3-8B-Instruct codes (mean of layer 30 of 40, chosen by the dev retrieval criterion) the VSA store
beats the best dev-chosen competitor in R1 by +6.06 [+5.04, +7.10] points (Qwen3-8B +6.77; e5 +0.60, tie).
- SUPPORT for the mechanism, not for a general law: after centring, the Granite codes' alias-label geometry is close to
  Qwen3-8B's (alias-label cosine median 0.53 vs 0.51; exact SimHash-signature agreement at k = 8: 0.08 vs 0.08; e5 0.12),
  consistent with the depth-matched, shared lexical/entity phase reported across families [KORS25, CDK+25, WS25].
  The win replicating on a second, independently trained family is in line with these reports.
- CONFLICT / caution: the best layer differed by family (Qwen3-8B 1/4 depth, Granite 3/4, Llama 3/4, Mistral PromptEOL at
  3/4), as [LLW+26] and [LWZ+24] warn; Granite's raw codes carry rogue dimensions [TvS21, MGBB25] that centring removes.
  Two LLM families do not establish the result for LLM codes in general; [NSK+26] finds alias robustness varies across
  13 LLMs. The e5 tie stays the counterexample: what predicts the margin is the code geometry, not "LLM" as such.
- GAP unchanged: no prior work compares a superposed VSA store with key-free classical memories at equal bits on more
  than one LLM family's hidden states (nearest [BNLSP26] trained VSA probes, [DH26] one model); B32 / B36 / B46 are the
  only such comparison we know of.
