## Conclusion
Up to 8 days of record (about 70k tokens), neither frontier model falls behind the read-then-replay pipeline on set-exact
accuracy. The registered bars that test that (Z1, Z2) fail for both models. What separates them is not state tracking
but three operational failures of the frontier arm: answers that use up the reasoning budget (gpt K = 4: 13%; Opus xhigh
K = 8 warehouse: 11/12), transport artefacts (glued answers), and output format (underscore IDs). Opus also loses item-level
recall at K = 8 in the deepest-nesting scenario (biobank). The pipeline itself degrades with K (0.93 -> 0.80 -> 0.71), mostly
in warehouse aisle-closure questions, where reader errors accumulate across days.
The errors are complementary: at K = 8, pipeline or gpt-repaired is right on 58/69 (0.84); at K = 1 on 0.99.
As the prereg stated in advance for this outcome, the pipeline's case at these scales rests on cost (USD 0 vs about
USD 0.2-0.5 per question for gpt-6.1-sol at K = 8), latency, on-device operation, traceability and the absence of
budget-exhaustion failures, not on accuracy.

## Literature check (conclusion -> literature)
PARTIAL CONFLICT: TLDM (arXiv 2505.14925, abstract read): none of seven frontier LLMs keeps a stable understanding of a novel's
storyworld configuration beyond 64k tokens. Here two 2026 reasoning models track state at a mean of 70k tokens as well as a
replay pipeline. Differences: structured operational logs, not prose; reasoning models; questions about the final state.
CONSISTENT: "The Deterministic Horizon" (arXiv 2606.00376, abstract read): extended chain-of-thought can degrade
deterministic state tracking. Here more thinking (Opus xhigh) lowered set-exact and micro-F1 at K = 8 through budget
exhaustion, though it helped biobank.

news coverage; not used.

Why the routes differ. For the same prompt text, Sidrune's reported prompt_tokens are 0.72-2.71 times OpenAI's (median
1.13 at every K). One tokenizer cannot give that spread on identical text, so Sidrune's "gpt-6.1-sol" is a different
model or deployment, or its usage figures are not the model's. Quality differs as well as budget: on the questions where
Sidrune finished (no budget exhaustion), the direct route is right on 13 that Sidrune (repaired + normalised) got
wrong and wrong on 1 that Sidrune got right at K = 4 (140 questions); at K = 8, 9 vs 4 (69). Sidrune's runaway-reasoning tail (K = 4: 21 of 161 calls at
32k) does not occur on the direct route (largest K = 4 call 13,174 tokens).
Correction to the conclusion above. Two of the three "operational failures of the frontier arm" (budget exhaustion,
glued answers) belong to the Sidrune route, not to gpt-6.1-sol. The third (underscore IDs) was Opus's and is unaffected.
On the official route, gpt-6.1-sol matches the pipeline on 1-day records and beats it on 4- and 8-day records (by 0.12 and
0.15, CIs excluding 0). The pipeline's case is therefore cost (USD 0 vs about USD 0.02 / 0.07 / 0.15 per question at
K = 1 / 4 / 8 on the direct route), latency, on-device operation and traceability, not accuracy, and at K >= 4 it is
less accurate. The Opus 5.5 arm (Anthropic direct) is unaffected by the route question.
Literature check (route finding -> literature). CONSISTENT: model substitution by API resellers is a known, hard-to-detect
problem. Cai et al. (arXiv 2504.04715) formalise substitution detection in LLM APIs and find output-based tests weak
against quantised or randomised substitution. Zhu et al. (arXiv 2506.06975) audit black-box APIs with a rank-based test.
Both were read only through their abstracts and secondary summaries. Our evidence is of a different kind and simpler: inconsistent token
accounting on identical prompts, plus a measured accuracy and reasoning-length difference on a fixed task. It does not
identify what Sidrune serves.
The finding also sharpens the earlier PARTIAL CONFLICT with TLDM (arXiv 2505.14925): on the official route a 2026
reasoning model tracks state over 8-day, about 70k-token operational logs better than a replay pipeline.
