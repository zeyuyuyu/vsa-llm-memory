B41 literature check: superposed multi-hop inference in VSAs vs table-based alternatives
Compiled 2026-11-18. Every item below was opened (arXiv abstract page, publisher/DOI page, or the full PDF text);
quoted numbers come from the paper text, not from memory. Tags are relative to our prior:
  PRIOR = per-hop crosstalk grows with stored facts (D ~ linear in items), noise compounds over hops without
  clean-up, clean-up costs E*D per hop, so sparse exact / top-k-merged tables should dominate on cost; VSA's real
  advantages are fixed width, bilinearity (all pairs in one bind), and differentiability.
Tag counts: SUPPORTS 14, CONFLICTS 5, GAP 9 (28 items).

Not verified directly: Plate's 2003 book (CSLI). Its capacity formula is cited here only as Frady et al. 2018
reproduce it (item 8). Everything else below was opened and checked.


GROUP 1. COMPUTING IN SUPERPOSITION / SUPERPOSED INFERENCE

1. Zhao, Q., Pandey, N. P., Tian, Y., Rosing, T. (2026). Superposed Inference for Hyperdimensional Computing.
   arXiv:2609.32320 (cs.LG, submitted 26 Sep 2026).
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32320   (EXISTS; this is the paper the user named)
   What it actually is: SupHDC, which batches K independent classification queries. Each query gets a random slot key,
   the keyed queries are superposed before one shared HDC encoding, and slot-specific classifiers read each one out.
   There is no binding chain, codebook clean-up, multi-hop, or uncertain-symbol propagation. Reported cost: 1.39x
   analytical speedup with no mean accuracy loss (K=2 with 20% fallback); 2.08x with -2.67 pp; 2.01x wall-clock on a
   Raspberry Pi 5. Without fallback, at D=10,000, accuracy falls -1.09 / -4.21 / -7.11 pp at K=2/3/4. Cross-slot
   interference is ~sqrt((K-1)/d) (Prop. 1). At K=4 the loss shrinks from 8.31 pp (D=1,000) to 2.67 pp (D=10,000).
   Tag: GAP. It does not address multi-hop or table baselines. Mechanistically consistent with the prior: interference
   grows with the number superposed, and a clean re-encode fallback is needed to hold accuracy.

2. Kleyko, D., Davies, M., Frady, E. P., Kanerva, P., Kent, S. J., Olshausen, B. A., Osipov, E., Rabaey, J. M.,
   Rachkovskij, D. A., Rahimi, A., Sommer, F. T. (2022). Vector Symbolic Architectures as a Computing Framework for
   Emerging Hardware. Proceedings of the IEEE 110(10):1538-1571. arXiv:2106.05268.
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05268  (also https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10588678)
   Calls "computing in superposition" the distinguishing feature of VSAs. Cross product of two sets in one bind:
   7 ops vs 29 brute force. It also says "computing the cross product in the symbolic domain might still require lower
   computational costs", and that for retrievable elements "the dimensionality ... should be proportional to the
   product of the sets' cardinalities".
   Substring search in superposition (an automaton run step by step): "required dimensionality ... grows extremely
   fast with the length of strings since every step ... introduces additional crosstalk noise". Per-step clean-up
   p_j = S S^T p_j (item memory, i.e. codebook-size x D per step) cuts D sharply but raises complexity from O(|Q|) to
   O(|Q||B|). The authors add that "its computational complexity exceeds that of the conventional algorithms
   optimized for the problem."
   Tag: SUPPORTS (strongly). This is the field's own statement of our per-hop crosstalk / clean-up cost trade-off.
   Bilinearity (all pairs in one bind) is confirmed as the genuine advantage.

3. Frady, E. P., Kleyko, D., Kymn, C. J., Olshausen, B. A., Sommer, F. T. (2021). Computing on Functions Using
   Randomized Vector Representations. arXiv:2109.03429 (no journal ref on the abs page).
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03429
   Vector Function Architectures: inner products of fractional-power-encoded vectors are similarity kernels, and VSA
   ops become function-space ops (density estimation, kernel regression). This gives the formal sense in which a
   weighted bundle is a kernel/function. It makes no cost comparison with tables and covers no multi-hop.
   Tag: GAP.

4. Kleyko, D., Rachkovskij, D. A., Osipov, E., Rahimi, A. (2022). A Survey on Hyperdimensional Computing aka Vector
   Symbolic Architectures, Part I: Models and Data Transformations. ACM Computing Surveys 55(6). arXiv:2111.06077.
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06077
   Reference survey of models, capacity, and data structures (including HRR knowledge-graph embeddings such as HolE).
   A full-text search finds no mention of sparse matrices, belief propagation, TensorLog, or the forward algorithm.
   Tag: GAP (background; no comparison with exact probabilistic propagation).

5. Kleyko, D., Rachkovskij, D. A., Osipov, E., Rahimi, A. (2023). A Survey on Hyperdimensional Computing aka Vector
   Symbolic Architectures, Part II: Applications, Cognitive Models, and Challenges. ACM Computing Surveys 55(9).
   arXiv:2112.15424.
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15424
   VSA finite-state automata keep the transition function as a superposition, and each step returns "next state +
   noise", which is then queried against the item memory. That is per-step clean-up, as in our design. "Due to the
   capacity limitations of HVs ... achieving deterministic behavior depends on several design choices" (dimension,
   number of atomic HVs, number of arguments superposed). No cost comparison with tables.
   Tag: GAP (weakly consistent with the prior).

6. Kanerva, P. (2009). Hyperdimensional Computing: An Introduction to Computing in Distributed Representation with
   High-Dimensional Random Vectors. Cognitive Computation 1:139-159. doi:10.1007/s12559-009-9009-8.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s12559-009-9009-8
   Defines item memory = clean-up memory. Its "job is nearest-neighbor search among the set of stored ... patterns",
   because many operations "produce approximate or noisy results that require cleaning up". Also: "the capacity of a
   single binary vector sets a limit on the length of history that it can represent reliably."
   Tag: SUPPORTS (clean-up is a search over the codebook, so its cost is about E*D).


GROUP 2. CAPACITY / CROSSTALK THEORY (how D scales with bundled items and codebook size)

7. Plate, T. A. (1995). Holographic Reduced Representations. IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks 6(3):623-641.
   doi:10.1109/72.377968.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1109/72.377968  (full text checked)
   Number of stored pairs k >= n / (16 ln(m^2/q)); numerically n ~ 4.5(k+0.7) ln(m/(30 q^4)), where n = dimension,
   m = number of candidates, q = error probability. Dimension is linear in stored items and logarithmic in codebook
   size. Intermediate clean-up "gives stronger results at the expense of introducing intermediate cleanup
   operations"; without it "the chances of error are higher". Decoding deeper structures is slightly worse; unitary
   cues remove that.
   Tag: SUPPORTS.

8. Frady, E. P., Kleyko, D., Sommer, F. T. (2018). A Theory of Sequence Indexing and Working Memory in Recurrent
   Neural Networks. Neural Computation 30(6):1449-1513. doi:10.1162/neco_a_01084. arXiv:1803.00412.
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00412
   Retrieval sensitivity is universal across VSA models: s = sqrt(N/M) (N = dimension, M = items superposed).
   "Achieving large sensitivity for high-fidelity recall requires the number of memory items to be smaller than the
   number of neurons." Capacity bound is 0.39 bits/neuron in the high-fidelity regime. Methods 4.2.2 reproduces
   Plate's (2003) linear relation N ~ 8 M log(D/eps) for alphabet size D.
   Tag: SUPPORTS (D linear in stored items, log in codebook).

9. Thomas, A., Dasgupta, S., Rosing, T. (2021). A Theoretical Perspective on Hyperdimensional Computing. Journal of
   Artificial Intelligence Research 72. doi:10.1613/jair.1.12664. arXiv:2010.07426.
   URL: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/12664
   With subset-incoherent random codes, decoding a bundle of s items from alphabet m needs d = O(s ln m). From
   worst-case pairwise incoherence alone, d scales "quadratically with the number of elements in the set, but only
   logarithmically in the alphabet size". Bound-pair incoherence scales only with log(m x n).
   Tag: SUPPORTS.

10. Clarkson, K. L., Ubaru, S., Yang, E. (2023). Capacity Analysis of Vector Symbolic Architectures.
    arXiv:2301.10352 (no venue on abs page).
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10352
    MAP-B bundle membership needs m = O(n log(d/delta)) (Thm 16); a bundle of key-value bindings needs the same
    (Thm 20); sequences of length L need m = O(L n log(Ld/delta)) (Thm 18). Here n = items bundled and d = codebook
    size. Lemma 17: for nested (depth-r) MAP-B bundling, reliability "decays exponentially with the depth". The paper
    explicitly links VSAs to sketches and Bloom filters.
    Tag: SUPPORTS. Linear-in-items D, plus a formal compounding result for repeated non-linear bundling.

11. Steinberg, J., Sompolinsky, H. (2022). Associative Memory of Structured Knowledge. Scientific Reports 12:21808.
    doi:10.1038/s41598-022-25708-y.
    URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9759586
    Binarized HRR structures of L relations decode accurately when N >~ O(L log D) (D = dictionary size), with SNR
    ~ N/L. Storage capacity is P < alpha_c N structures.
    Tag: SUPPORTS.

12. Kleyko, D., Bybee, C., Huang, P.-C., Kymn, C. J., Olshausen, B. A., Frady, E. P., Sommer, F. T. (2023).
    Efficient Decoding of Compositional Structure in Holistic Representations. Neural Computation.
    doi:10.1162/neco_a_01590. arXiv:2305.16873.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16873
    Decoders from sparse coding and compressed sensing, combined with interference cancellation, raise information
    rate from 1.20 to 1.40 bits/dim (small codebooks) and from 0.60 to 1.26 bits/dim (large codebooks).
    Tag: CONFLICTS (mild, constant-factor only). A naive single-pass clean-up understates achievable capacity, so the
    per-item D constant in our prior is pessimistic. But these decoders are iterative (cost > one E*D pass), and the
    scaling stays linear in items.

13. Qiu, F. (2022; v5 2023). Graph Embeddings via Tensor Products and Approximately Orthonormal Codes.
    arXiv:2208.10917.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10917
    This is the only head-to-head analysis we found of a superposed (bind-and-sum) graph memory against sparse matrix
    formats. Embeddings "need to scale with the number of edges k". At best (tensor-product binding) they "match"
    DoK / COO / CSR in memory (O(k)) and in matrix-multiply cost (O(k^2)); there is no asymptotic win. Hadamard
    (HRR-family) binding of dimension d stores O(d) edges for a single-edge query but only O(sqrt d) edges for edge
    composition (2-hop path). The paper concludes: "We actually do not save any memory by compression via the
    Hadamard product".
    Tag: SUPPORTS (strongly). Composition in compressed binding is worse than single-hop: D ~ k^2 for 2-hop.


GROUP 3. RESONATOR NETWORKS (factorizing bound products without enumeration)

14. Frady, E. P., Kent, S. J., Olshausen, B. A., Sommer, F. T. (2020). Resonator Networks, 1: An Efficient Solution
    for Factoring High-Dimensional, Distributed Representations of Data Structures. Neural Computation
    32(12):2311-2331. arXiv:2007.03748 (arXiv title: "Resonator networks for factoring distributed representations
    of data structures").
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03748
    Decoding a product of factors by nearest-neighbour lookup "would require storing every possible combination".
    Past VSA work "sidestepped this problem by limiting the depth ... or using a brute force approach". Resonators
    start from the superposition of all codevectors and alternate unbinding with clean-up, so they never enumerate
    the product space.
    Tag: CONFLICTS, but only with a k^h-enumeration framing. VSA does not need to enumerate the product space to
    decode one bound composite. It does not address weighted superpositions of many chains or compare to factorized
    exact propagation.

15. Kent, S. J., Frady, E. P., Sommer, F. T., Olshausen, B. A. (2020). Resonator Networks, 2: Factorization
    Performance and Capacity Compared to Optimization-Based Methods. Neural Computation 32(12):2332-2388.
    arXiv:1906.11684 (arXiv title: "Resonator Networks outperform optimization methods at solving high-dimensional
    vector factorization").
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11684
    Explicit brute-force comparison: storing all composites needs M*N bits. With F=4 factors, 100 codevectors each,
    and N=10,000, M=1e8, which is "about 125 GB". Resonator operational capacity M_max (99% accuracy) "scales as a
    quadratic function of N, regardless of how many factors". The method is "dramatically" better than ALS and
    gradient methods but "not guaranteed to converge".
    Tag: CONFLICTS with "VSA = enumeration cost". The win holds against brute force over the joint space, for a single
    clean product. Search space is capped at ~N^2. There is no comparison with factorized/sparse exact inference.


GROUP 4. PROBABILISTIC / SOFT INFERENCE WITH VSAs; MULTI-HOP AND REASONING IN SUPERPOSITION

16. Furlong, P. M., Eliasmith, C. (2023, issue 2024). Modelling Neural Probabilistic Computation Using Vector
    Symbolic Architectures. Cognitive Neurodynamics 18(6). doi:10.1007/s11571-023-10031-7. Extends Furlong &
    Eliasmith (2022), Fractional Binding in Vector Symbolic Architectures as Quasi-Probability Statements, CogSci 2022
    (https://escholarship.org/uc/item/33j7c63v).
    URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11655797
    Bundles of fractionally bound HRR/SSP vectors define a quasi-kernel density (a Fourier integral estimator). It can
    go negative and needs a bias-and-rectify correction. Belief update is vector addition, memory is fixed (vs KDE),
    and D=2048 is used in experiments. "None of the candidate methods exactly achieve the exact value of entropy."
    No multi-hop chains, no table baseline.
    Tag: GAP (probability-in-superposition is established but approximate; no cost comparison).

17. Ganesan, A., Gao, H., Gandhi, S., Raff, E., Oates, T., Holt, J., McLean, M. (2021). Learning with Holographic
    Reduced Representations. NeurIPS 2021. arXiv:2109.02157.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02157
    "HRRs today are not effective in a differentiable solution due to numerical instability." A projection step
    improves concept retrieval by over 100x. Applied to multi-label output layers.
    Tag: GAP. It qualifies the "differentiability" advantage: HRR needs projection or normalization to be usable
    inside gradient training.

18. Hersche, M., Zeqiri, M., Benini, L., Sebastian, A., Rahimi, A. (2023). A Neuro-Vector-Symbolic Architecture for
    Solving Raven's Progressive Matrices. Nature Machine Intelligence 5(4):363-375. doi:10.1038/s42256-023-00630-8.
    arXiv:2203.04571.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04571
    Each perceptual PMF is mapped to "the weighted superposition with the values in the PMF used as weights and the
    corresponding codewords as basis vectors". This is exactly our softmax-to-bundle encoding. Rule abduction then runs
    by bind/unbind in superposition. On the 3x3 grid (2000 tests, CPU), NVSA is 244x faster than unrestricted PrAE
    (63.2 min vs 15,408 min), uses <10 GB vs >53 GB, and scores 96.89% vs 97.50% accuracy.
    Tag: CONFLICTS. This is the strongest published cost win for superposed probabilistic inference. Caveat: the
    baseline is PrAE's exhaustive probabilistic search, not a factorized/sparse exact method, and accuracy is 0.6 pp
    lower.

19. Kumar, R. (2026). Holographic Memory for Zero-Shot Compositional Reasoning in Knowledge Graphs: A Mechanistic
    Study of Where and Why It Fails. arXiv:2606.24948 (single author, preprint).
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24948
    HRR and FHRR with modern-Hopfield clean-up on FB15k-237 (two-hop, leakage-controlled, 5 seeds). Single-hop filtered
    MRR is 0.358, but zero-shot two-hop composition "stays at chance across all cleanup temperatures". The hop-1
    intermediate is recovered (MRR 0.896), yet the second hop fails: "a capacity and interference effect present
    already at a single hop", "not the bind-unbind algebra or the cleanup".
    Tag: SUPPORTS (per-memory crosstalk is the binding constraint for multi-hop HRR retrieval).

20. Liu, Y., Chung, W. Y., Chen, H., Yeung, C., Imani, M. (2025; v2 Feb 2026). Encoder-Free Knowledge-Graph
    Reasoning with LLMs via Hyperdimensional Path Retrieval (PathHD). arXiv:2512.09369.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09369
    Relation paths are encoded as block-diagonal GHRR hypervectors (non-commutative binding), retrieved by blockwise
    cosine plus Top-K pruning, then answered with one LLM call. Reports 40-60% lower latency and 3-5x less GPU memory
    than neural-encoder / multi-call LLM pipelines, with Hits@1 matching or better on WebQSP, CWQ, GrailQA.
    Tag: GAP. Baselines are neural encoders, not sparse/symbolic traversal, and the method itself relies on a Top-K
    (table-like) prune.

21. Zhu, H., Hao, S., Hu, Z., Jiao, J., Russell, S., Tian, Y. (2025). Reasoning by Superposition: A Theoretical
    Perspective on Chain of Continuous Thought. NeurIPS 2025. arXiv:2505.12514.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12514
    With companion: Hao, S., Sukhbaatar, S., Su, D., Li, X., Hu, Z., Weston, J., Tian, Y. (2024). Training Large
    Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space (Coconut). COLM 2025. arXiv:2412.06769.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769
    "Each continuous thought vector is a superposition state that encodes multiple search frontiers simultaneously."
    Graph reachability takes D steps (D = diameter) vs O(n^2) decoding steps for the best known constant-depth
    discrete CoT. Coconut's continuous thoughts "encode multiple alternative next steps", which amounts to BFS.
    Tag: CONFLICTS with the argmax-per-hop baseline only: carrying a superposed frontier beats committing to one
    path. The comparator is discrete CoT, not an explicit sparse frontier vector, and width/capacity limits are not
    the subject.

22. Zhang, Z., He, X., Yan, W., Shen, A., Zhao, C., Wang, S., Shen, Y., Wang, X. E. (2025). Soft Thinking:
    Unlocking the Reasoning Potential of LLMs in Continuous Concept Space. arXiv:2505.15778.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15778
    Training-free "concept tokens" = "probability-weighted mixture of token embeddings", i.e. the LLM's softmax is
    carried forward as a superposition. Up to +2.48 pass@1 and -22.4% tokens vs CoT on math/code.
    Tag: GAP. This is the closest LLM-side analogue of our uncertain-reading superposition, but there is no binding,
    no external multi-hop memory, and no table baseline.


GROUP 5. CLASSICAL / TABLE BASELINES AND COST COMPARISONS

23. Cohen, W. W. (2016). TensorLog: A Differentiable Deductive Database. arXiv:1605.06523; and Cohen, W. W.,
    Yang, F., Mazaitis, K. R. (2020). TensorLog: A Probabilistic Database Implemented Using Deep-Learning
    Infrastructure. JAIR 67:285-325. doi:10.1613/jair.1.11944.
    URLs: https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06523 ; https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/11944
    Exact probabilistic deduction by unrolled belief propagation (as matrix ops). "Inference is linear in database
    size and the number of message-passing steps." It runs on GPU at hundreds of thousands of triples.
    Tag: SUPPORTS. Exact propagation is linear in hops, with no k^h, and differentiable too, so differentiability is
    not unique to VSAs.

24. Cohen, W. W., Sun, H., Hofer, R. A., Siegler, M. (2020). Scalable Neural Methods for Reasoning with a Symbolic
    Knowledge Base. ICLR 2020. arXiv:2002.06115.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06115
    Weighted entity sets are k-hot vectors, and follow(x, r) = x M_R is a sparse matvec whose support is exactly the
    R-neighbours. In the 2-hop benchmark, a key-value memory network (dense embedding memory, one slot per triple,
    dim 64) is about 7x slower at 50 relations / 10k entities and runs out of memory beyond 200k entities or 100
    relations. The reified KB handles 10x the entities and 20x the relations; it scales to tens of millions of facts.
    Tag: SUPPORTS (direct cost evidence: sparse exact propagation beats a distributed embedding memory).

25. Sun, H., Arnold, A. O., Bedrax-Weiss, T., Pereira, F., Cohen, W. W. (2020). Faithful Embeddings for Knowledge
    Base Queries (EmQL). NeurIPS 2020. arXiv:2004.03658.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03658
    Query-embedding systems (Query2Box) are not logically faithful: models "do not have the capacity to model all the
    information in a large KB accurately, unless embeddings are impractically large." EmQL's fix is to represent
    sets as a centroid plus a count-min sketch, decoded via Top-k MIPS and then the sketch, which is a sparse /
    sketch table.
    Tag: SUPPORTS.

26. Rabiner, L. R. (1989). A Tutorial on Hidden Markov Models and Selected Applications in Speech Recognition.
    Proceedings of the IEEE 77(2):257-286. doi:10.1109/5.18626.
    URL: https://doi.org/10.1109/5.18626  (full text checked)
    Direct enumeration of state sequences costs on the order of 2T*N^T; the forward procedure costs N^2*T (N=5, T=100:
    about 3000 vs about 10^72 operations). Exact per-step marginalization removes the k^h blow-up entirely.
    Tag: SUPPORTS. The right exact baseline is per-hop marginalization (forward / sparse matvec), not enumeration.
    Most published VSA cost wins (items 14, 15, 18) are measured against enumeration.

27. Zhang, J., Zhang, X., Yu, J., Tang, J., Tang, J., Li, C., Chen, H. (2022). Subgraph Retrieval Enhanced Model for
    Multi-hop Knowledge Base Question Answering. ACL 2022. doi:10.18653/v1/2022.acl-long.396. arXiv:2202.13296.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13296
    "Since the top-1 relevant path cannot be guaranteed to be right, we perform a top-K beam search at each time."
    The top-K trees are then unioned and identical entities merged.
    Tag: SUPPORTS (argmax-per-hop is fragile; top-k beam with merging is the standard table remedy).

28. Chen, H., Ni, Y., Zakeri, A., Zou, Z., Yun, S., Wen, F., Khaleghi, B., Srinivasa, N., Latapie, H., Imani, M.
    (2024). HDReason: Algorithm-Hardware Codesign for Hyperdimensional Knowledge Graph Reasoning. arXiv:2403.05763.
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05763
    HDC knowledge-graph completion on FPGA: 10.6x speedup and 65x energy efficiency vs RTX 4090; 4.2x / 3.4x vs an
    FPGA GCN. It does not do multi-hop and makes no comparison with sparse or symbolic traversal.
    Tag: GAP (an HDC cost win, but against GNNs, not tables).


SYNTHESIS
- Capacity theory agrees across Plate 1995, Frady 2018, Thomas 2021, Clarkson 2023, and Steinberg & Sompolinsky
  2022: required D grows linearly with the number of superposed items and only logarithmically with codebook size.
  Composition is worse. Hadamard/HRR binding holds only O(sqrt d) edges for 2-hop composition (Qiu), and nested
  bundling decays exponentially with depth (Clarkson Lemma 17). Multi-step superposed computation without clean-up
  needs D that "grows extremely fast" with steps, while per-step clean-up costs codebook x D (Kleyko et al., Proc
  IEEE). This matches our prior.
- Every published case where superposition wins on cost (NVSA 244x vs PrAE, resonators vs a 125 GB brute-force
  table, the 7-vs-29-op cross product, continuous vs discrete CoT) compares against enumeration of a product space or
  against single-path commitment. None compares against factorized exact propagation (forward algorithm, sparse
  matvec, TensorLog), which already avoids k^h (Rabiner: N^2 T vs 2T N^T).
- The two direct comparisons with sparse structures go against VSA. Qiu shows at best parity with DoK/COO/CSR in
  memory and multiply cost for tensor binding, and a loss for HRR-family binding. Cohen et al. 2020 and EmQL find
  sparse/sketch methods faster and more faithful than dense embedding memories. HRR 2-hop composition on FB15k-237
  is at chance (Kumar 2026).
- The documented advantages are the ones we listed: fixed width, bilinearity (all pairs in one bind), end-to-end
  differentiability (NVSA), and parallel or in-memory clean-up that makes the E*D step O(1) in time on suitable
  hardware (Proc IEEE). Differentiability is not unique, since TensorLog and the reified KB are differentiable too,
  and HRR needs a projection step to train stably (Ganesan).
- Open gap: we found no paper that, on the same multi-hop task with uncertain (softmax) symbol readings, measures
  accuracy together with ops, memory traffic, and state size for weighted-superposition VSA vs argmax per hop vs
  top-k beam with merging vs exact sparse forward propagation. Also missing: how peaked vs flat reading
  distributions change effective crosstalk, and in which regime (dense frontiers, small per-relation memories, or
  in-memory hardware) VSA could break even. B41 would fill this gap.
