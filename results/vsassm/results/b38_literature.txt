# B38 literature check (before the run; each source read in its own text unless marked "abstract/index only").
# Question: does a VSA memory need fewer cells than an error-protected table for the same exactness under device
# noise and localized damage? Items are tagged by what they predict for B38; the result-side reading is appended
# after the test run (section "After the result").

== A. Error-correcting codes for phase-change / resistive memory (the tables' side)

[XZ11] Xu, W. & Zhang, T. A time-aware fault tolerance scheme to improve reliability of multilevel phase-change memory
  in the presence of significant resistance drift. IEEE Trans. VLSI Syst. 19(8), 1357-1367 (2011). Text: binary BCH
  codes protecting 4 kB pages, BCH(34816, 32768, 128), BCH(36864, 32768, 256), BCH(30960...), and length-34816 rate-0.94
  and length-36864 rate-0.89 quasi-cyclic LDPC codes; "conventional use of ECC ... can only achieve the performance with a
  big gap from the information-theoretical bounds"; with static sensing "simulation results clearly show very abrupt
  PER drops over the time".
  -> Precedent for BCH and LDPC as the standard protection of PCM storage (B38's E-BCH, E-LDPC), at high code rates
     (0.89-0.94) because PCM raw error rates there are small. Also documents the ECC "cliff": long codewords fail
     abruptly once errors exceed the design point. Predicts: at low cell-error rates a high-rate code costs ~1.1x the
     information bits, far below any VSA store (which needs tens of cells per fact even with no errors).

[SLSB10] Schechter, S., Loh, G. H., Strauss, K. & Burger, D. Use ECP, not ECC, for hard failures in resistive
  memories. ISCA 2010. Text: PCM wear-out produces permanent "stuck-at" cell faults that are "immediately detectable, at
  write time"; ECP "corrects errors by permanently encoding the locations of failed cells into a table and assigning
  cells to replace them", with lower overhead than ECC for the same lifetime (e.g. ECP6 in 61 bits per 512-bit row,
  11.9%).
  -> Stuck cells in real resistive memories are typically known to the controller (erasures), which is far cheaper
     to correct than unknown errors. B38's primary localized damage keeps locations UNKNOWN to every reader (the
     harsher, VSA-friendly assumption, Kanerva's "irrespective of position"); a secondary condition gives every reader
     the locations.

[BWW09] Baeg, S., Wen, S. & Wong, R. SRAM interleaving distance selection with a soft error failure model. IEEE Trans.
  Nucl. Sci. 56(4), 2111-2118 (2009) (index and abstract only): models multi-cell upsets as compound Poisson clusters
  and chooses the bit-interleaving distance so that one cluster hits at most one bit per SEC word.
  -> Interleaving ECC words across the array is standard memory practice against clustered/localized faults; supports
     giving the ECC tables a (random) interleaver in B38's primary damage conditions.

[PPV10] Polyanskiy, Y., Poor, H. V. & Verdu, S. Channel coding rate in the finite blocklength regime. IEEE Trans. Inf.
  Theory 56(5), 2307-2359 (2010) (abstract/index): at blocklength n and error probability e the best rate is about
  C - sqrt(V/n) Q^-1(e). -> Short per-fact codes (13-17 information bits) pay a large finite-length penalty; long
  codes over many facts approach capacity. Predicts that long BCH/LDPC tables gain most where the VSA (one fact per
  similarity decision) cannot amortize redundancy, i.e. at low noise.

== B. Robustness of HD/VSA representations (the VSA side)

[Ka09] Kanerva, P. Hyperdimensional computing: an introduction to computing in distributed representation with
  high-dimensional random vectors. Cogn. Comput. 1, 139-159 (2009). Text (section "Robustness"): "for maximum
  robustness ... the information encoded into a representation should be distributed 'equally' over all the components
  ... When bits fail, the information degrades in relation to the number of failing bits irrespective of their
  position. This kind of representation is referred to as holographic or holistic"; "Error-correcting codes of data
  communications are robust in the sense that they tolerate some number of errors. A remarkable property of
  hyperdimensional representation is that ... the proportion of allowable 'errors' increases with dimensionality."
  -> The classic claim B38 tests: position-independent, graceful degradation. B38's natural-layout damage conditions
     (a vector stored contiguously, damage localized) test "irrespective of their position"; the interleaved
     conditions test whether an ECC table given an interleaver obtains the same property.

[K20] Karunaratne, G. et al. In-memory hyperdimensional computing. Nat. Electron. 3, 327-337 (2020) (arXiv:1906.01548):
  "HDC is remarkably robust to random variability and device failures"; "deterministic spatial variations in the
  conductance values could pose a challenge"; their remedy: "dividing the crossbar array into f equal sized partitions"
  and placing the segments "spatially distributed across the crossbar array", because "given the holographic nature of
  the hypervectors, this can be easily addressed by a random partitioning approach"; accuracy "increases with the
  number of partitions".
  -> Even the in-memory HDC prototype spreads hypervectors across the array to survive spatially correlated
     variation, i.e. it interleaves. Supports giving interleaving to both sides; predicts that a contiguously stored
     hypervector is NOT immune to block-level (contiguous) damage.

[Kl22] Kleyko, D. et al. Vector symbolic architectures as a computing framework for emerging hardware. Proc. IEEE 110,
  1538-1571 (2022) (read for B34): HDC tolerates "8.8x higher probability of failures with respect to intermittent
  hardware errors, and 60x higher ... permanent hardware errors" than unprotected baselines.
  -> Comparisons against unprotected baselines; silent on ECC-protected baselines at equal storage.

[R17] Rahimi, A. et al. High-dimensional computing as a nanoscalable paradigm. IEEE TCAS-I 64, 2508-2521 (2017) (read
  for B34): HD classifier tolerates an 8.8-fold failure probability vs a baseline without redundancy. Same reading.

[ZW21] Zhang, S., Wang, R., Zhang, J. J., Rahimi, A. & Jiao, X. Assessing robustness of hyperdimensional computing
  against errors in associative memory. ASAP 2021 (abstract): "HDC is considerably robust to errors in the associative
  memory"; robustness depends on data width; random error injection only.

[Xu23] Xu, W. et al. HyperMetric: robust hyperdimensional computing on error-prone memories using metric learning.
  ICCD 2023 (text): argues ECC "is not cost-effective due to the large implementation overhead. For example, CIM-SECDED
  [Crafton et al. 2021] sacrifices 20% memory cells".
  -> CONFLICT-TO-TEST: the claim that ECC costs too many cells is made without an equal-cell comparison; B38 measures
     cells per fact for both.

[MAC24] Mejri, M., Amarnath, C. & Chatterjee, A. Error resilient hyperdimensional computing using hypervector encoding
  and cross-clustering. VTS 2024 (text): HDC associative memories are "vulnerable to timing and soft errors"; TMR costs
  "x3 times"; proposes checksum hypervectors. -> HD memories themselves are protected with redundancy in practice;
  no equal-storage ECC comparison.

== C. HD codes as channel codes; capacity of superposition (what to expect for cells per fact)

[FKS18] Frady, E. P., Kleyko, D. & Sommer, F. T. A theory of sequence indexing and working memory in recurrent neural
  networks. Neural Comput. 30, 1449-1513 (2018). Text: high-fidelity capacity of superposition (bundling) memories
  "asymptotic capacity value is 1/(4 ln 2) = 0.36" bits per neuron, "0.39" with their approximation; without
  superposition "it is possible to achieve the theoretically optimal capacity of 1 bit per neuron and ... crosstalk
  immediately limits the achievable capacity".
  -> Predicts that even without noise a VSA store needs ~1/0.36 = 2.8x (and with 1-bit sign storage more, about
     2.8 x pi/2 = 4.4x) the cells of a table holding the same information bits: the referee's capacity point.

[Ki18] Kim, H.-S. HDM: hyper-dimensional modulation for robust low-power communications. ICC 2018; [HK23] Hsu & Kim,
  IEEE Trans. Commun. 71(3) (2023); [He21] Hersche, M., Lippuner, S., Korb, M., Benini, L. & Rahimi, A. Near-channel
  classifier: symbiotic communication and classification in high-dimensional space. Brain Inform. 8, 16 (2021) (text):
  superposed HD vectors as a channel code "achieving a similar SNR gain compared to state-of-the-art codes" (LDPC,
  Polar) at short block lengths (D = 256-512) and BER 1e-4 to 1e-5.
  -> SUPPORTS that superposed random codes decoded by similarity can match LDPC/Polar codes at short blocks and low
     rates. Predicts B38's VSA can be competitive at high noise (low rates), where codes of a few hundred cells per
     fact are needed anyway.

[JB12] Joseph, A. & Barron, A. R. Least squares superposition codes of moderate dictionary size are reliable at rates up
  to capacity. IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 58(5) (2012) (arXiv:1006.3780 abstract): superposition codes with least-squares
  decoding reach capacity. -> Superposition per se need not cost capacity with optimal (joint) decoding; the VSA's
  one-item-at-a-time similarity decoding is not that decoder.

[TDR21] Thomas, A., Dasgupta, S. & Rosing, T. A theoretical perspective on hyperdimensional computing. J. Artif. Intell.
  Res. 72, 215-249 (2021) (abstract): non-asymptotic conditions for bundles to be decodable; no ECC comparison.

== D. Equal-storage comparison of HD memories with ECC-protected storage

Searched (Nov 2026): "hyperdimensional computing versus error correcting codes equal memory", "hyperdimensional fault
tolerance compared to TMR or ECC at the same memory overhead", "hypervector spatially correlated / clustered faults,
crossbar row/column failure". Found: none. The closest are equal-rate channel-code comparisons of superposed HD
modulation (Ki18, He21, HK23) and HD robustness studies against unprotected baselines (Kl22, R17, ZW21). The only
spatial-variation study (K20) handles it by spreading hypervectors (interleaving). Gap: no published measurement of
cells per fact for an HD/VSA memory against BCH/LDPC-protected storage, nor of HD memories under localized faults at a
matched error rate against interleaved ECC. B38 fills this; the paper should say "we found no precedent", not "first".

== After the result (B38 test: 50 RULER stores, 4,776 probed keys; numbers from results/b38_verdict.txt)

SUPPORTING
- [XZ11] (BCH/LDPC are the standard PCM protection; "very abrupt PER drops"): B38's coded tables need the fewest cells
  (E-BCH at low cell-error rates, E-LDPC with soft-combined repetition at high ones), and they fail abruptly past their
  design point: an E-LDPC table designed for p(k8) at 128 cells per fact reads 98.4% of keys at eps = 0.24 and 12.6% at
  0.276 (M3).
- [K20] (hypervectors spread across the crossbar to survive spatial variation): B38 shows why this matters and that it
  is what ECC interleaving does. With a random interleaver every memory sees localized damage as i.i.d. errors (H3); a
  VSA vector stored contiguously already spreads each fact over its d cells, which is why it tolerates dead lines and
  clusters in the natural layout (H4).
- [Ka09] ("degrades in relation to the number of failing bits irrespective of their position"): holds for the VSA store
  against dead lines and stuck clusters in the natural layout (V-last within 0-3 grid steps of its i.i.d. requirement),
  and does NOT hold for contiguous loss (whole vectors lost; every memory loses about the lost fraction of its facts).
- [FKS18] (superposition capacity 0.36-0.39 bits per component in the high-fidelity regime; crosstalk costs capacity
  that codes without superposition keep): with no cell errors the VSA store needs 76 cells per fact for 99.5% exact
  reads, about 0.17 information bits per cell (12.6-bit values), against 19 cells (0.66 bits per cell) for a table;
  a table of random HD codes without superposition (T-vhdd) needs 32. The referee's capacity objection is confirmed:
  the RULER stores at 16 pairs per vector were over-provisioned 6.7x.
- [Ki18, He21, HK23] (superposed HD codes competitive with LDPC/Polar at short blocks and low SNR): partly - the VSA
  store needs 2.0-2.4x the cells of the best LDPC table at the highest noise (k16, k32), its closest approach, against
  4x with no errors; HD codes are better channel codes at low SNR than at high SNR, but not better than LDPC with soft
  repetition.

CONFLICTING
- [Xu23] ("ECC ... is not cost-effective due to the large implementation overhead"; HD as the cheaper path to
  robustness): at equal stored cells, for storing key-value facts, B38 finds the opposite: tables with BCH or LDPC need
  2-4x fewer cells than the VSA store at every device-noise level from none to 32x (H1), and 11-19 grid steps (7-27x)
  fewer than a VSA whose item memory is stored on the cells (H1cb). (Their claim concerns HDC classifiers and
  circuit overhead, not stored cells; B38 does not count decoder logic.)
- [Kl22, R17] (HD tolerates 8.8x / 60x higher failure rates than baselines): qualified - true against unprotected or
  repetition-coded tables (the VSA store needs 2x fewer cells than B34's repetition table T-rep at k8-k32), false
  against BCH/LDPC tables.
- [He22] (hash structures fragile under memory errors): a hash directory protected by BCH or LDPC is not fragile; the
  best tables in B38 keep a minimal-perfect-hash directory on the noisy cells.

GAP (unchanged from the pre-result search): no published equal-cell comparison of an HD/VSA memory with ECC-protected
storage, nor of HD memories against interleaved ECC under localized faults at a matched error rate. B38 is, as far as
we found, the first; the paper should say "we found no precedent".

Not searched further: decoder logic area/energy (B38 counts stored cells only), multi-level cells, and soft (analog)
reads, any of which could change the comparison.
