Reading of the results (written after the run):
- Mapping. The real-valued mapping the referee asked for is the bipolar store: every FHRR phase rounded to 0 or pi gives
  a +-1 code, binding is a sign flip, and each stored or codebook component is one differential pair of PCM devices.
  The ideal 1-bit store reproduces B22 amendment 2 on every store (4,776 / 4,776, asserted per store).
- With global drift compensation (one scale per array, Joshi et al.'s GDC), the store with BOTH the stored vectors and
  the clean-up codebook in PCM crossbars reads all 4,776 probed keys exactly at every time from 60 s to 1 year, at the
  RULER load (16 pairs per vector) and at 32, for both the 1-bit (B1) and the multi-level (ML) form. At 64 pairs per
  vector the ideal stores already miss keys (B1 4 keys, 0.9992; ML 1 key, 0.9998) and the PCM arms stay within 0.07
  points of them (lowest: B1-C/SC 0.9985 at 30 days; ML-SC 0.9992 at 1 year).
- Which stage limits. At the measured parameters, with GDC, neither: the B1 store read made no bit errors at any time
  (25 uS against a RESET device; the sign survives the 54% conductance decay of a year), and the ML store read carried
  an rms error of 0.25-0.30 of rms(m) (B22's read-noise scale r ~ 0.3, against B22's exact range r <= 2). The clean-up
  similarity averages device errors over 8,192 components, so they add only ~0.004 to it. Without compensation the
  limiting stage is the clean-up for B1 (B1-S reads signs, which drift does not change; B1-C/SC fall to 99.54% at 1 h,
  51.8% at 1 day and 0% at 30 days, because the common decay, GDC alpha 0.95 -> 0.46 from 60 s to 1 year, pulls a match
  similarity of ~0.8 under the fixed 0.5 threshold), and both stages for ML (ML-SC: 95.4% at 1 h, 0% at 1 day). This is
  the failure Joshi et al. report for a network without compensation (accuracy "quickly decreases down to 10% ...
  within approximately 1000 s"), and the same one-scale-per-array calibration removes it.
- Margin (descriptive; all device noise sources multiplied by k, 30 days, 16 per vector, GDC). B1-SC stays exact up to
  k = 4 (0.9996) and fails at k = 8 (0.0029). Alone, the B1 store survives k = 8 (0.985, about 12% bit errors) and fails
  at k = 16; the clean-up fails at k = 8 (0.246). A post hoc check on one store (not registered) shows why: at large k
  the RESET devices' clamped noise adds conductance that the summed-current GDC counts as signal but the differential
  output cancels, so GDC over-estimates the gain (true gain / GDC alpha = 0.988 at k = 1, 0.86 at k = 4, 0.58 at k = 8,
  0.09 at k = 16) and every match similarity is scaled toward or under 0.5. The clean-up's break at k = 8 is therefore
  the calibration rule, not the averaging; a calibration on differential reference columns would remove that bias (not
  tested). The ML form breaks earlier and at the store read (ML-S 0.900 at k = 4, ML-C 1.000 at k = 4), in line with
  multi-level storage being the weak point of PCM (Le Gallo & Sebastian 2020). ADC: 6 bits exact for both forms; 4 bits
  exact for B1 and failing for ML (0.0015), because the registered full-scale tile ADC leaves ML tile outputs below one
  step (a range set from the output distribution, as Joshi et al. do for activations, was not tested). Read noise
  redrawn on every MVM instead of once per time point: identical (1.0000 both forms).

Deviations from the pre-registration: none in conditions, metric, threshold or bars. Prediction P3 was wrong in one
sub-item (ML-C without compensation read 81.2% at 30 days against a predicted < 80%). Times start at 60 s rather than
1 s because the published model is defined from T1 = 55.39 s (stated in the prereg). The GDC gain-bias check above is
post hoc and labelled so.

Limits: a simulation of a published statistical model of one PCM technology (IBM's 90-nm prototype chip), not a
hardware run; the model was fitted to data up to 1e5 s, so 30 days and 1 year are extrapolations. Not modelled: IR drop,
stuck or defective devices (B22's failed-component conditions, up to 40% of components zeroed with no loss, bound this),
deterministic spatial variation (Karunaratne et al. counter it by spreading each vector across the array), temperature,
ADC nonlinearity. The energy figures are per-operation estimates from the cited papers, not a design.

Literature check (conclusion -> literature):
- Supporting. Karunaratne et al. 2020 stored binary hypervectors (d = 10,000) in PCM crossbars for associative search,
  with on-chip accuracy comparable to software; Karunaratne et al. 2021 stored bipolar/binary high-dimensional keys in
  more than 256,000 PCM devices for few-shot learning close to 32-bit software accuracy; Joshi et al. 2020 needed a
  global per-array scale (GDC) to stop drift from collapsing accuracy. Our results agree on all three points.
- Qualifying. Le Gallo & Sebastian 2020 name drift variability "arguably the most significant challenge for multi-level
  storage in PCM"; our multi-level store is indeed the weaker form (larger read error, breaks at k = 4 where the 1-bit
  store holds), yet at the measured parameters it still reads exactly, because a read averages 8,192 components and the
  store tolerates read noise up to r = 2 (B22). Joshi et al. (Supplementary Fig. 6) find that a ResNet-32 with GDC
  tolerates a drift-exponent spread up to about 0.013 over 30 s to 1 year (criterion: accuracy above 90%); our 1-bit
  store with GDC stayed exact at k = 4 at 30 days (spread 0.017 on programmed devices, with programming and read noise
  also x4). The time spans differ (30 days here), so this is only a rough indication that the store is at least as
  tolerant as that network under the same compensation.
- New relative to these precedents: they classify into at most 22 classes (or 5-way few-shot); here the clean-up has up to
  5,993 candidates, a fixed acceptance threshold and a stop rule, and the stores were filled by a language model reading
  a real long-context benchmark.
